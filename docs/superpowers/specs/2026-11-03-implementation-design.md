# SIAPSO Mother-Child: implementation design

Status: approved for implementation on 2026-11-03 (the team asked for autonomous delivery, choosing the most rigorous option at each decision).
Scope: Jira SCRUM-15 to SCRUM-44. Product context: `PRODUCT.md`. Agent rules: `CLAUDE.md`.

This document is the single technical reference for every ticket. When a ticket and this document disagree, this document wins and the ticket is updated.

French interface sentences are quoted here without accents for brevity. The interface and the knowledge pack always use correct French spelling, with accents (for example "Allez au poste de santé maintenant.").

Each phase ends with a live test round (section 12). One ticket is one branch and one pull request.

Work that needs people or accounts we do not have is delivered as far as code allows and reported plainly:

- clinical sign-off of rules (pharmacist and midwife): rules ship as "not clinically validated";
- volunteer voice recordings with consent (SCRUM-32): harness and public-dataset baseline only;
- Hugging Face publication (SCRUM-33) and hosting (SCRUM-36): scripts and instructions, publication by the team;
- ARP report format (SCRUM-41) and DHIS2 district mapping (SCRUM-43): provisional formats, clearly labelled;
- GPU fine-tuning with consented data (SCRUM-42): scripts and plan;
- making the repository public (SCRUM-44): the audit is done, the visibility change waits for the team.

## 2. Decisions on gaps and contradictions in the dossier

| Topic | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red for medicines | Red (`danger`) is reserved for danger signs. A product rule may use `danger` only with a written `danger-rationale` (immediate vital risk); the starter base used it once: castor oil at term with a uterine scar (reference case 9); the live test round 1 (SCRUM-49) added misoprostol with a uterine scar and misoprostol late in pregnancy, at least as high as castor oil, for the committee to confirm. The interface names a product danger "Danger : a montrer maintenant", never "Signe de danger". | Arbitration "red = danger signs only", with the explicit committee exception of case 9. |
| ANSM interaction levels | Contre-indication and association deconseillee map to `before-next-dose`; precaution d'emploi and a prendre en compte map to `next-contact`. | Red is reserved; orange is the highest product level. |
| Evidence cap | A finding with evidence D, E or 0 is capped at `next-contact` unless the rule kind is `toxicity`. The pack build fails when a rule declares a level above its cap, so nothing is capped silently; the engine caps again as a second barrier. | Dossier rule, made explicit. |
| Evidence B at orange | Allowed (reference case 6). The "B to D" wording of the level table is descriptive, not a rule. | Only D, E and 0 are capped. |
| Composite evidence ("C/D for toxicity, E for interaction") | One rule per risk, each with one evidence level. | Keeps the cap computable. |
| Woman mode content | Shows all four levels, the level name and pictogram, the risk sentence without numbers, the orientation, the fixed sentences, the knowledge base version and "not clinically validated". Hides the detailed fact, the evidence level and the sources. | Dossier arbitration D1; the woman still sees the version. Confirmed after the live test round 2 (SCRUM-50): the details are written for a professional and the orientation carries the urgency (docs/intended-use.md, "What a result shows in each mode"). |
| Accompanied mode content | Everything in woman mode, plus fact, detailed risk, evidence level with its wording, sources, rule identifier (these five behind one closed "Pourquoi ?" disclosure per card, SCRUM-50), reminders and contraception information. | Dossier; the result stays short for a slow reader (live test round 1). |
| Known product, no rule triggered | Grey level. A product that no rule reads gets the "nothing found" message: "Rien n'a ete trouve sur ce produit dans notre base. Notre base ne dit pas si ce produit peut faire du mal. Montrez cet ecran a la sage-femme ou au pharmacien a la prochaine visite." A product that a rule reads, when no rule applies to the situation, gets "nothing applies": "Notre base ne contient rien sur ce produit pour cette situation. Notre base ne dit pas si ce produit peut faire du mal dans cette situation." with the same orientation (the base is silent, which says nothing of what others know), a gap and never an all-clear. The version and date of the base are not put inside the sentence: the result says them near the top and at the end (SCRUM-50). | Never implies safety; keeps four levels; never says "nothing found" about a product the base has a rule on (live test round 1); said positively, so a listener who hears only the end never hears "sans risque" (live test round 1, SCRUM-50). |
| Fixed sentences | Every result ends with: "Ne changez rien a votre traitement sans en parler a un professionnel." then the version line, then "Cet outil ne remplace pas un soignant." | One canonical variant (annex C). |
| Wording | Allowed verbs: montrez, dites, parlez-en, demandez, allez, appelez (show, tell, talk, ask, go, call). Not used: "N'arretez rien", "Verifiez", "autorise" (they contain forbidden words). | Consistency with the forbidden list. |
| Forbidden words test | French: sur (the adjective sûr), sans danger, verifie, verifiez, arretez, ne prenez pas, remplacez, diminuez, prenez plutot, autorise, compatible. English: safe, harmless, verified, verify, stop, do not take, replace, reduce, take instead, authorised, authorized, compatible, no risk. Whole-word, accent-aware, case-insensitive, over every pack message and every interface string. "sans risque" is allowed only inside "ne veut pas dire que c'est sans risque" / "does not mean it is risk-free"; the pack and the interface no longer use even that (SCRUM-50), and the app wording test refuses both phrases on the result of every reference case. | Dossier lists, applied to both languages. |
| Orange timing | Default orientation is "avant la prochaine prise" (before the next dose); a rule may give its own orientation, never later than its level: a long-term treatment says "Parlez-en rapidement ... sans rien changer en attendant" (cases 6 and 8 included), never "dans les prochains jours". A danger orientation always says now. `siapso-pack check` refuses a later timing. | Cases 6 and 8; live test round 1 (one urgency everywhere). |
| Weeks | Weeks of amenorrhoea (SA). Windows: `lt12` (0 to 11), `12-19`, `20-29`, `30-plus`. | Clinical convention in Senegal and France. |
| Term input | Last menstrual period date, weeks, months, or unknown. Months and unknown give a range of weeks; the engine triggers every rule whose windows intersect the range (riskiest window per rule) and flags the finding "terme estime" or "terme inconnu". The engine function `rangeFromMonths(m)` covers weeks 4.33 x (m - 1) + 2 to 4.33 x m + 2 (the month in progress). Because "enceinte de m mois" can mean the month in progress or months completed, the app maps a months answer to the union of both readings: from the start of month m to the end of month m + 1 (to 42 for month 9), precision estimated. Weeks, months and unknown answers are stored with the date they were given (`givenOn`) so the term advances between visits (an older record without it is dated by the profile's `updatedAt`); an LMP answer is a date and advances by itself. A whole-week answer stands for that week and its 6 days: "19 weeks" is 19+0 to 19+6, so one day after it was given its upper end reaches 20 (weeks keep precision exact). The weeks answer asks "Ce terme date de quand ?" with "Aujourd'hui" or "Un autre jour" (a date): a new number clears that answer, which is then asked again (always in accompanied mode, and in woman mode on an edited profile; a new profile of her own keeps "Aujourd'hui"), and a day other than today reads back both ("Environ 32 semaines aujourd'hui (26 semaines le 24/09)"). Unknown is the range 0 to 42. The month field takes two digits and refuses anything outside 1 to 9; 10 to 12 is never read as weeks (she may count lunar months, or be overdue): the screen asks "Elle a depasse 9 mois ?" ("Avez-vous depasse 9 mois ?" in woman mode) with "Oui, plus de 9 mois" (past 42 weeks, below) and "Non, ce sont des semaines" (the weeks field, empty, typed by hand); 13 to 42 is said to look like weeks, with one tap to give it "En semaines"; month 8 or 9 suggests weeks or the last period date instead. A new number of weeks that clears the day answered says so ("Le nombre de semaines a change : redites de quand date ce terme."). Nothing typed is ever cut short. Past 42 weeks and still pregnant is a kind of term of its own (`post-term`, with the day it was said): it comes from "Non, toujours enceinte" on an outdated known term, from a last period or weeks given another day that put the term today past 42+0 weeks (up to 44+6; from 45 weeks the date is said to be looked at, "une grossesse ne dure pas si longtemps"), or from "Oui, plus de 9 mois"; it is never one of the choices (a mis-tap would drop the early windows). It reads as 42 weeks and over (precision estimated, "Plus de 42 semaines"), never as unknown, and the app never tells her to answer "Je ne sais pas" to a term she knows. Wherever it shows (the profile or passer-by questions, the check, the result) the app says "Allez au poste de sante aujourd'hui : une grossesse de plus de 42 semaines doit etre vue." ("Elle doit aller..." in accompanied mode) with "Voir qui appeler", and the check goes on. It holds on its day, the baby may be born from the next day (possibly past term), and after a week (`POST_TERM_HOLDS_DAYS`, 7) it is outdated and the question is asked again. | Solves "riskiest window when it differs per substance". |
| Outdated and possibly past term | `profileTermStatus(profile, today, notBefore)` (app, `domain/term.ts`) is the one place that says where a pregnancy stands, in days of amenorrhoea: not pregnant; ok; possibly past term (not blocking): the upper end of the term is past 42+0 weeks while its lower end is not (for example month 8 or 9 after a few days, weeks 41 or 42 given another day), and the answer was not given today (a term given today, 42 weeks included, is never "to confirm": she was seen pregnant that day), or an unknown-term or "Je ne sais pas" answer is older than 28 days (`UNKNOWN_HOLDS_DAYS`); the words of the range say its real upper end ("Entre 41 et 51 semaines (estime)"), which explains the possible birth, while the rules keep it held at 42; outdated (blocking): the lower end of the term is past 42+0 weeks, an unknown-term or "Je ne sais pas" answer is older than 42 weeks, or a stored value cannot be read; clock: the phone's date is before a day the app has already seen (the profile's `updatedAt`, its last history entry, the knowledge pack's date) or before the day a term was given, for every kind of term, the last period included. Possibly past term: the check runs with the pregnancy range held at 42 weeks and breastfeeding added with no baby age and no prematurity (every breastfeeding rule applies); the result says "Nous ne savons pas si le bebe est ne : ce resultat tient compte de la grossesse et de l'allaitement." ("si votre bebe" in woman mode) and the profile list says "A confirmer : le bebe est peut-etre ne" with "Mettre a jour" as the way to her profile ("A verifier", first proposed, is not used: no form of "verifier" appears in the interface). Outdated: a required step before any check and as a guard on the result: "Le terme note a passe 42 semaines. Le bebe est-il ne ?" (woman mode: "Votre terme note a passe 42 semaines. Votre bebe est-il ne ?"), with "Oui, le bebe est ne" (the profile opens with the pregnancy set to no and the breastfeeding and baby age questions to answer; "Allaite : Non" then says plainly that the app covers pregnancy and breastfeeding, with the way to delete the profile, and "Enregistrer" is hidden while that answer stands, so no button does nothing) and "Non, toujours enceinte" (on a known term, the profile opens with the term past 42 weeks and the advice to go to the health post today; on an unknown term, with the term to give again); both come back to the check. "Mettre a jour" on the list asks the same question first ("Le terme note a peut-etre passe 42 semaines. Le bebe est-il ne ?" when the baby may only be born), never a plain form, and "Non, toujours enceinte" there opens the term to give again ("Donnez le terme d'aujourd'hui."). When the phone's date is before the profile's last save, its form says "La date du telephone semble fausse ..." and shows no term worked out from it, a date after the phone's date is said to be the phone's ("Regardez la date du telephone"), and the result's notice leads with the same words. No result is worked out on an outdated profile (`contextFromProfile` throws `OutdatedTermError`); the list says "A mettre a jour". Clock: the term is read as unknown and, the real date being later, breastfeeding is added as for a possibly past term; the result's notice says why and that the baby may be born. Whenever the baby may be born (`babyMayBeBorn`: possibly past term, outdated, clock), the danger signs now screen asks about the signs after the birth and of the baby up front with those of the pregnancy, from the same status and the same days already seen as the check. The danger signs and who to call stay in the header. The result reads the profile and the date again when the app comes back to the front. | A pregnancy profile is kept across visits: after the birth it would hide every breastfeeding rule; a possible birth is not a reason to block a check. |
| Pregnancy not known | "Enceinte ?" offers "Je ne sais pas": stored `pregnant: true, pregnancyUnsure: true` with the day it was said (`pregnancyUnsureOn`, kept while not answered again), read as a pregnancy of unknown term (0 to 42, every window) and dated like an unknown term (possibly past term after 28 days, outdated after 42 weeks); the breastfeeding questions are still asked, and the screens say "Grossesse possible, terme inconnu". | Unknown is not safe. |
| Baby age not known | "Age du bebe" offers "Je ne sais pas": stored `babyAgeUnknown: true` with no age; the engine then applies every age-bounded rule, as for the youngest babies. | Unknown is not safe. |
| Term look-ahead | The engine looks `TERM_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS` (14) past the upper end of the term for the rules that give a finding: windows are those of [weeksMin, weeksMax + 2]. It only adds windows; the level stays the highest of the rules that apply. Reminders and notes keep the term as it is. The app and the API run the same engine, so they agree. A conservative engineering default (section 13). | A course of treatment goes on over the coming days: ibuprofen at 19 weeks is still taken at 20. |
| Profiles on a shared phone | A pseudonym is used once on the phone (trimmed, compared without case or accents) and has at most 40 characters (the limit is said, never cut). The list, the check and the result show the term used in plain words ("Environ 32 semaines", "Entre 6 et 15 semaines (estime)", "Terme inconnu", "Allaite, bebe de 3 mois"), so the right woman and term are seen. "Supprimer ce profil" (profile screen, then a last step) deletes one profile and its history in one transaction, under the code guard as every write of profile data (`requireCode`, NoCodeError from a tab whose code is no longer the one stored); a result kept after its profile was deleted is not written. A deletion reaches the other tabs (live test round 2): its id on the channel only (a tab that missed it finds only a new epoch token and takes the quick exit); they forget her list and her answers not saved and leave her screens once they read her gone; a profile read from storage is saved back only while its record is there, checked in the write's transaction (`ProfileGoneError`). A pseudonym that says a dose or a forbidden word is refused (it heads the result); one a letter from another (punctuation and spaces aside) or like a first and family name is said, never refused; a sentence ending on a pseudonym with its own stop keeps one ("Awa."). Woman mode opens her own profile only: a profile made in woman mode is marked `self: true`, one made in accompanied mode `self: false`, and an older unmarked record is hers only when it is the only profile; after she deletes hers, an empty profile of her own opens even when other profiles stay (docs/privacy.md). A save, a confirmation, a deletion or "Voir le resultat" tapped twice runs once. | Live test round 1 (SCRUM-45). |
| Lists of a visit (accompanied mode) | Choosing another woman keeps the unfinished list of the woman left in memory, per profile (items, names typed, lists of unrecognised names, "none of these" answers; not the danger signs, asked again), and gives it back when she is chosen again; her row says "Liste en cours : n produits". The lists waiting are forgotten at every lock of the tab (`forgetOthers`: two minutes idle or hidden, a lock or another code from another tab), the list on screen staying in memory (with the screen held after its own two minutes); every list goes at the quick exit, the erase and a reload; a deleted profile takes its own (`forget`). Never in storage nor on a history entry (docs/privacy.md). | Live test round 2 (SCRUM-50): a relay moving between women in one compound lost each list without a word. |
| Look up without saving (accompanied mode) | "Regarder sans enregistrer" on the list of profiles: the questions of a profile without a pseudonym (pregnant with "Je ne sais pas", term, breastfeeding and baby age, situations), read with the same term rules as a profile (an unknown or uncertain answer takes the riskiest windows); the answers are a profile kept in memory only (`CheckState.quick`), with no history entry and nothing stored. A new one always starts empty. Shown in accompanied mode only, she is let go when another woman or passer-by is looked at, at every lock of the tab (`forgetQuick`; the code then opens the list of profiles, her screen is not held), at any change of mode, at the quick exit, at the erase and at a reload. An outdated term opens her questions again (`updateQuick` keeps her list), never a profile screen. "Femme suivante" on her result lets her go and opens empty questions for the next one; a check with no woman chosen in accompanied mode opens the list of profiles, never a new profile (Back after a lock). Her form is warned about before the auto-lock ("ses reponses seront effacees"), and the next screen says once that they were cleared. The situations are asked too: a uterine scar makes castor oil at term a danger (reference case 9). | Live test round 2 (SCRUM-50): a depot keeper needed 16 to 20 taps and a stored profile per customer seen once. |
| Breastfeeding | Baby age in weeks and a prematurity flag; rules may bound the age or require prematurity. | Dossier silent on bands. |
| Danger signs | One fixed list for everyone: the dossier list (fever, bleeding, convulsions, severe headache, baby no longer moving), the case signs (very strong abdominal pain; baby sleeps much more than usual, feeds poorly, breathes with difficulty), completed with WHO danger signs cited as fact-only sources. Every item carries its source and stays "not clinically validated" until the committee aligns it with the national list. | Missing a danger sign is the worst failure. |
| Current danger signs in a check | The check flow asks once "Has she one of these signs now?" before the result. The question always opens with nothing ticked, never with an earlier answer (even on Back from the result); "Aucun de ces signes" tapped while signs are ticked asks first ("Retirer les signes coches (...) ?", the safer answer "Non, garder ces signes" focused); a change to the list asks it again; what is ticked goes into the check as it changes, so the browser's Forward to the result shows a sign ticked after Back, and a result with no answer asks the question again. | Cases 2 and 10 need the fever; "now" may have changed since the last answer. |
| Paracetamol maximum (C7) | Optional "units per day" for products that have a maximum-dose rule; the engine sums strength x units per day per substance and compares with the threshold stored in the rule with its source. The message never states a dose. The paracetamol generic has strength variants (`paracetamol-500-generic`, `paracetamol-1000-generic`) in its resolver family, so the confirm screen can offer the strength. When units per day are given for a product of unknown strength, the engine adds the note `max-dose-not-computable` ("La quantite totale ne peut pas etre calculee sans le dosage. Montrez les boites a la sage-femme ou au pharmacien."), shown in both modes, never a level. | MVP check without giving doses; the default path (typing "paracetamol") must not silently skip the check. |
| Preventive calendar (C10) | Reminders ("demandez si vous avez recu...") are separate from levels and shown in accompanied mode only; the IPTp reminder is never shown to a woman living with HIV on cotrimoxazole: neither when the condition `hiv-cotrimoxazole` is entered nor when a checked item contains sulfamethoxazole (rule key `unless-with`, reference case 15). | Dossier; a woman may enter her cotrimoxazole as a medicine without the condition. |
| Contraception (C9) | General information notes only (formulation 8), accompanied mode only, never a verdict. | Decree 2023-2419. |
| Codes | Every profile has a 4 to 6 digit code. A code among the first ones tried (one digit repeated, digits in a row up or down, a block said again or each digit doubled, the keypad's columns, a day and a month DDMM or MMDD, a six-digit date DDMMYY, MMDDYY or YYMMDD, a year from 1950 to 2030) gets a plain warning ("Ce code est facile a deviner", not to use a date or a year of birth) and a second yes ("Garder ce code"), never a refusal (live test round 2, SCRUM-50 and SCRUM-53). "Changer le code" (Reglages, behind the code) checks the current code first as its own counted try, then asks the new one twice with the same warning, then wraps every data key for the new key and stores its verifier in one transaction; a failure leaves the old code working, and the other tabs lock. "Quitter" is offered before the code on every screen that opens without it and says what the app is for (danger signs, who to call, settings, about, knowledge base, licences, voice), never on the code screen. What happened, and a deleted profile's id, travel only on the BroadcastChannel; local storage and IndexedDB keep one random epoch token, written again at every event, start and unlock, which stores no meaning: a tab that finds a token it did not hear about takes the quick exit and reads the stored code again. A record is written only under the code it was sealed with (the vault holds the key and its code's fingerprint together). A danger result is warned about 30 s before the auto-lock, as answers not saved are. Five wrong attempts lock for 30 seconds, doubling each time, up to 30 minutes; the wait is shown in minutes and seconds ("Attendez 1 min 59 s") and said in words to a screen reader ("1 minute 59 secondes"), and the keypad key that takes the last digit off says "Corriger", never the word EFFACER of the forgotten-code erase. Auto-lock after 2 minutes without interaction. Quick exit locks immediately and shows a neutral screen. The quick exit, a lock asked for and the forgotten-code erase reach every tab of the app (a BroadcastChannel, plus an epoch of random marks in local storage (and its storage event) and in IndexedDB, which each tab checks with the stored code on focus, visibilitychange and pageshow, for a frozen or cached tab); every tab locks and forgets its held screen, or returns to the welcome after the erase. An auto-lock stays in its own tab. A code is stored only when none is (a tab left on the welcome asks for the stored code instead of making a second one, and says a code exists), and opens the app only once storage shows it stored; a welcome cut short after the code (onboarded false, a code stored) shows the code screen, then asks the mode; profile, history and contact writes, and the mode and welcome in the settings, go through only with the code the tab opened (its verifier's salt and hash, compared in the write's transaction). A tab follows an erase or a new code only once the stored code shows it. Danger signs ticked without the code are forgotten after 2 minutes without interaction or hidden, at every lock and at a reload, and the quick exit is offered on the danger signs and who to call before the code. What screens leave on history entries stays in memory; the entry holds only marks. The answers of a profile or contact form not saved yet are kept in memory only (never stored) across an auto-lock and given back to the same screen after the code, said not saved; the quick exit, a lock from another tab, the erase, another code or leaving the screen drop them; while they are not saved, a band warns 30 s before the auto-lock, and a touch puts it off (WCAG 2.2.1); a passer-by's answers are warned about too, then go with her at the lock. Details: `docs/privacy.md`. | Shared phones; mobile money users know 4-digit codes. Live test round 1 (SCRUM-48): a second tab stayed open after "Quitter" and could write a profile back after the erase. |
| App name on the home screen | Build-time setting, default "Carnet" (neutral) with a neutral icon. | Dossier asks for a neutral name. |
| Phone numbers | No personal number in the repository: only the public national emergency numbers of Senegal (SAMU 1515, fire brigade 18) in `app/src/config/emergency.ts`, shown on every who to call screen with "Allez au poste de sante ou au centre de sante le plus proche."; the deployment file may replace the numbers and adds its circuit text under that line; the team confirms them before a pilot. Referral contacts are entered on the phone behind the code, in both modes, or provided by a deployment file `referral.json` that is not committed (an example file is). The app page answered for that file (HTML) is read as no file, a valid file loads whatever its content type, the service worker never answers `/config/` with the app page, and a production build without the file, with invalid JSON or with entries the phones would leave out prints a loud warning. | Tickets SCRUM-26 and SCRUM-46: a danger must always lead to a number to call. |
| Open data threshold | A cell is published only when its count is greater than 10. | Literal reading of "au-dela de 10", the stricter option. |
| Server stack | One Node.js service in TypeScript that imports the same engine package. No FastAPI layer. | Parity by construction, one runtime, fewer moving parts. The dossier's FastAPI gateway can be added in front later without changing the engine. |
| Signing | Ed25519 with `@noble/ed25519` (works on older Android browsers where WebCrypto Ed25519 is missing). | Dossier lists noble-ed25519. |
| Profile encryption | One PBKDF2-SHA-256 derivation per unlock (600 000 iterations, random 16-byte salt), split with HKDF-SHA-256 into the stored verifier check and a non-extractable wrapping key kept in memory only while open. Envelope encryption: every profile, history entry and the saved contacts are sealed with AES-GCM under their own random 256-bit data key, wrapped by that key (random 96-bit IVs, additional data binding the envelope version, the store and the record id); the phone's one code (hers in woman mode, the accompanying person's in accompanied mode) opens every profile of the phone. The key is forgotten at every lock, the quick exit, a lock from another tab, the erase and another code. The first right code after the update seals what was kept in clear and stores a new verifier in one transaction. The situations of a saved profile, a note on contraception and the note of an HIV-related situation are shown at a tap only. The threat model (casual access, a lost phone or storage read without the code; not a forensic attacker who tries every code offline) is in `docs/threat-model.md`. | Ticket SCRUM-39. A key derived from the old verifier (the PBKDF2 output itself, stored) would be readable from storage; HKDF keeps one derivation per unlock. History and contacts hold names, levels, typed text and numbers: sealed too. |
| Interface languages | French and English. Wolof is supported in names and voice input, not as interface text. | Team decision; Wolof written interface undecided. |
| A name typed as unrecognised that the base knows (live test round of 2026-11-05) | "Je ne trouve pas" asks first when the text looks like products or remedies of either list; a name kept as typed is evaluated again as its best candidate of both lists, shown as a possible match, while the text stays unknown. | Free text must never give a lower result than the base knows (castor oil typed among the medicines, Advil typed as its box form). Models propose, rules decide: the engine sets the level of the possible match. |

## 3. Repository and packages

pnpm workspace, TypeScript strict everywhere, ES modules.

| Path | Package | Role | Depends on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| `tools/knowledge/` | `@siapso/pack-tools` (private) | Authoring schema, YAML loading, validation, provenance check, pack build, NOTICE generation, reference-case runner. | engine, resolver |

## 4. Knowledge model

### 4.1 Authoring format (YAML, validated by JSON Schema 2020-12)

Registry:

- `substances.yaml`: `id` (INN, kebab-case), `names` (`fr`, `en`), `classes` (class ids).
- `classes.yaml`: `id`, `names`, `excludes` (required, possibly empty: substance ids left out of the class, for example low-dose aspirin rules later).
- `products/<id>.yaml`: `id`, `names` (`fr`, `en`), `brands` (list: the brand of a branded product, or the brands people use for a generic, such as Coartem for artemether-lumefantrine), `local-names` (list of `text`, `lang` in `fr|wo|en`, `checked` boolean: validated by a speaker), `components` (at least one; `substance`, `strength-mg` optional), `form`, `route`, `source` (`bdpm` with CIS code, or `demo-manual`), `demo` boolean. `registry/products/bdpm.yaml` is a generated list of products; the other names people use for a brand ("Doliprane codeine" for CODOLIPRANE) are given to its products by the import (`LOCAL_NAMES`), as unchecked French local names.
- `plants/<id>.yaml`: `id`, `latin-name`, `names`, `local-names`, `parts`, `evidence` (overall), `message` (default message id for the grey state), `sources`.

Knowledge:

- `rules/<id>.yaml`: `id`, `kind` (`pregnancy`, `breastfeeding`, `interaction`, `herb-drug`, `toxicity`, `duplicate`, `max-dose`, `condition`, `reminder`, `contraception-info`), `when` (match, below), `level`, `evidence`, optional `danger-rationale`, `message` (message id), `sources` (list of `source` id from `sources.yaml`, `locator` (record title), `url`, `accessed` date, optional `quote`), `review` (`status` `draft|validated`, `reviewers` list of name and role, `reviewed-on`). A rule has no `licence` field: every content file is CC BY 4.0 through its first line `# SPDX-License-Identifier: CC-BY-4.0`.
- `when` keys (kebab-case, mapped to the engine `RuleWhen` of section 5.1): `subject` (`{ substance }`, `{ class }` or `{ plant }`, exactly one key; required except for `reminder`), `requires` (non-empty any-of list of `{ with: <subject> }`, another item, or `{ condition: <id> }`; an `interaction` or `herb-drug` rule needs a `with`), `unless-conditions` (the rule does not apply when one of these conditions is present), `unless-with` (non-empty list of subjects: the rule does not apply when any item matches one), `strength-above-mg` (substance subject only, not on `max-dose` or `duplicate`), `max-mg-per-day` (required for `max-dose`, only there), `pregnancy-windows` (non-empty list of window ids), `breastfeeding` (`baby-age-weeks-max`, `premature`).
- `messages/<id>.yaml`: per language `fr` and `en`: `fact`, `risk` (no numbers), optional `risk-detail` (may hold numbers, accompanied mode), optional `orientation` (overrides the level default).
- `danger-signs.yaml`: `id`, `group` (`pregnancy`, `postpartum`, `baby`), `label` (`fr`, `en`), `sources`.
- `sources.yaml`: `allowed` (id, name, publisher, licence, version or download date, url, notice text), `fact-only` (WHO documents: may support a fact, never copied text), `forbidden` (DDInter, LEDIAM, e-lactancia, CRAT, Theriaque, Vidal, full DrugBank: with the reason).
- `pack.yaml`: `version` (semver), `date` (ISO date of the content freeze), `clinically-validated: false`.

Condition ids: `hiv-cotrimoxazole`, `tuberculosis-treatment`, `epilepsy-treatment`, `uterine-scar`, `renal-failure`, `hepatic-failure`, `sickle-cell`.

### 4.2 Build and runtime pack

`pnpm knowledge:build` validates every file, checks provenance (every rule cites at least one `allowed` source, no `forbidden` id or domain appears anywhere), checks the evidence cap and the danger rationale, checks message ids and forbidden words, then writes `app/public/pack/`:

- `manifest.json`: `schema` (integer), `version`, `date`, `clinicallyValidated`, `counts`, `fragments` (path, sha256, bytes);
- fragments, in this order (`PACK_FRAGMENTS` of `@siapso/pack-tools`; a test of the app checks that its loader asks for exactly these): `substances.json`, `classes.json`, `products.json`, `plants.json`, `rules.json`, `danger-signs.json`, `sources.json`, `messages.fr.json`, `messages.en.json`;
- `pack.json`: the whole pack in one file, for tools and tests (not listed in the manifest).

There is no resolver index file: the app builds the resolver index on the device from the products, plants and substances of the pack.

The runtime format is typed by `@siapso/engine` (`Pack`); the build script is typed against it, but only in part. Type-checked: the products and plants (each field is mapped explicitly), the metadata (`version`, `date`, `clinicallyValidated`), the sources, and the keys of a rule `when` (a test compares the keys of the rule schema with `RULE_WHEN_KEYS`, which the engine types by `RuleWhen`). Checked at runtime only, by `parsePack` on the built pack (the build fails, naming the file): the messages, the top level of a rule (these are cast to `Rule`, `Message` and `DangerSign` after the camelCase conversion), and the danger signs. So a change to one of those engine types needs the matching schema and tests to be updated by hand. The build is deterministic (no clock: version and date come from `pack.yaml`); building twice gives identical bytes. Budget: under 3 MB gzip. The build also generates `NOTICE` from `sources.yaml`.

## 5. Engine

### 5.1 Contract

```ts
type Level = 'danger' | 'before-next-dose' | 'next-contact' | 'unknown';
type Evidence = 'A' | 'B' | 'C' | 'D' | 'E' | '0';
type WindowId = 'lt12' | '12-19' | '20-29' | '30-plus';
type TermPrecision = 'exact' | 'estimated' | 'unknown';

interface EvaluationContext {
  pregnancy?: { weeksMin: number; weeksMax: number; precision: TermPrecision };
  breastfeeding?: { babyAgeWeeks?: number; premature?: boolean };
  conditions: ConditionId[];
  items: CheckedItem[];      // confirmed by the user; long-term treatments included with longTerm: true
  signs: DangerSignId[];     // danger signs present now
}
type CheckedItem =
  | { ref: { kind: 'product' | 'plant'; id: string }; unitsPerDay?: number; longTerm?: boolean; strengthUnknown?: boolean } // strengthUnknown: the strength on the box is not known, component strengths read as unknown
  | { ref: { kind: 'unrecognised'; text: string } };

interface Finding {
  level: Level;
  check: CheckId;            // C1 to C12
  ruleId?: string;
  evidence?: Evidence;
  items: number[];           // indexes into context.items
  message: string;           // message id
  term?: TermPrecision;      // set when a pregnancy window matched on a range
  capped: boolean;
  sources: SourceRef[];
}
interface Evaluation {
  level: Level;              // highest finding level
  findings: Finding[];       // sorted: level (danger first), then check, then rule id
  reminders: Reminder[];     // C10, accompanied mode only
  notes: Note[];             // C9 (kind 'contraception', accompanied mode only) and max-dose-not-computable (kind 'dose', both modes)
  pack: { version: string; date: string; clinicallyValidated: boolean };
}
interface Reminder { ruleId: string; message: string; sources: SourceRef[] }
interface Note extends Reminder { kind: 'contraception' | 'dose' }  // the app filters on kind
function evaluate(pack: Pack, context: EvaluationContext): Evaluation;

type Subject = { substance: string } | { class: string } | { plant: string };
interface RuleWhen {
  subject?: Subject;                // required for every kind except reminder
  requires?: ({ with: Subject } | { condition: ConditionId })[]; // any-of
  unlessConditions?: ConditionId[]; // the rule does not apply when one is present
  unlessWith?: Subject[];           // reminder and contraception-info only: the rule does not apply when any item matches one
  pregnancyWindows?: WindowId[];
  breastfeeding?: { babyAgeWeeksMax?: number; premature?: boolean };
  maxMgPerDay?: number;             // max-dose only
  strengthAboveMg?: number;         // substance subject only
}
```

Level order: `danger` > `before-next-dose` > `next-contact` > `unknown`.

### 5.2 Checks

| Check | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| C1 pregnancy | Applies only to a pregnancy context; rule windows (all windows when none are listed) intersect the windows covered by the context range, its upper end moved on by `TERM_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS` (14 days). The look-ahead applies to every rule that gives a finding (windows are listed on C1 and C5 rules today); reminders (C10) and notes (C9) use the range as it is. |
| C2 breastfeeding | Applies only to a breastfeeding context, within its age bound and prematurity requirement. |
| C3 interaction | Subject and `with` both present among distinct items; a rule without a `with` partner is rejected. |
| C4 herb-drug | Same as C3 with a plant subject and a medicine partner. |
| C5 toxicity | Subject present, context conditions met; not capped by evidence. |
| C6 duplicate | Two or more distinct items share a substance (or a class) that has a duplicate rule. |
| C7 maximum dose | The known sum over items of strength-mg x units-per-day for the substance is greater than the rule threshold, even if some items lack a unit count or a strength (a known partial sum above the threshold is a certain excess); skipped only when the known sum is at or below the threshold. When the rule could apply and an item holding the substance has units per day but no strength, a note `max-dose-not-computable` (kind `dose`) is added (one per rule), never a level; it is not added when the same rule already triggered C7 (the finding is enough, and the note would contradict it). |
| C8 condition | Subject present and one of the conditions (or the `with` item) present. |
| C9 contraception | Produces a note (kind `contraception`), never a level. |
| C10 calendar | Produces a reminder, never a level. |
| C11 danger signs | Each present sign gives a `danger` finding with the standard danger message. |
| C12 not found | Each unrecognised item gives an `unknown` finding with the "not recognised" message. |

A product or plant with no finding gives an `unknown` finding: plants use their own grey message (evidence 0 wording); products use "nothing applies" (`nothing-applies`) when a rule of the pack that gives findings reads them (as its subject, whatever the strength threshold, or as a partner; not a reminder or a contraception note), else "nothing found". A plant with evidence 0 and no rule always gives grey. The app says, in both modes and before the cards, each situation of the profile that no rule of the pack reads.

### 5.3 Guarantees (tested)

- An unrecognised item always yields `unknown`, whatever else is present.
- No input yields a level outside the four levels; there is no "safe" output.
- Same inputs, same output (pure, deterministic order).
- Unknown term triggers every pregnancy rule for the substance.
- The term look-ahead never removes a window that applies today (`engine/test/lookahead.test.ts`).
- Invalid numbers in the context (non-finite or negative baby age, non-positive units per day) raise an error instead of silently lowering a level; the app validates input before calling the engine.
- Evidence D, E, 0 never exceeds `next-contact` except `toxicity`.
- Coverage of `engine/` at least 90 percent (lines, branches, functions, statements).

## 6. Resolver

- Normalisation: lowercase, NFD with diacritics removed, every character that is not a letter or a digit (apostrophes, hyphens) to a space, collapse spaces, then per word: `ph` to `f`, `qu` to `k` (quinqueliba, kinkeliba; nivaquine, nivakine), `y` to `i`, a doubled consonant to one, a trailing `e` dropped in words of more than 4 letters. `kh` is not mapped to `x`: it would change the meaning of Wolof names (dakhar stays dakhar).
- Stop-words: the words of pharmaceutical forms and packaging, the generic words medicament and emplatre, and the generic words of remedies (remede, plante, racine, ecorce, graine, tisane) (comprime, pellicule, secable, effervescent, orodispersible, gelule, capsule, sirop, solution, suspension, buvable, poudre, sachet, granules, creme, pommade, gel, collyre, suppositoire, injectable, ampoule, flacon, medicament, medicaments, medicamenteux, emplatre, emplatres, and English tablet, capsule, syrup, cream and the like, accented or not) are removed from indexed names and from the query; a query made only of stop-words gets no candidate ("racine" no longer reaches "Huile de ricin"). Carrier words name nothing either (huile, feuille, mousse, infusion, decoction, jus) and neither does "generique": with a linking word left alone at either end removed, "huile de nim" is neem and "huile de coco" reaches nothing. Box abbreviations of forms (cp, cpr, comp, eff, gel, sach, susp, sol, buv, supp, LP and the like) are stop-words too, read as the forms they stand for when they choose a form ("Efferalgan 1g cpr eff" gives the effervescent EFFERALGAN 1000 mg).
- Salt words: the salt or hydrate a box names next to the substance (sodium, sodique, potassique, chlorhydrate, hyclate, phosphate, sulfate, fumarate, maleate, hemihydrate and the like) are removed from names and queries with the "de" or "d'" that links them, so "valproate de sodium", "valproate sodium" and "chlorhydrate de tramadol" are read as "valproate" and "tramadol". A salt word is kept when it is the whole query ("sodium") or a substance of the registry ("calcium" if the registry had it); magnesium is a salt word ("valproate de magnesium"), and the valproate generic is also named "divalproate de sodium".
- Age words (adulte, enfant, nourrisson and their plurals, and English adult, child, infant) are not stop-words but carry no match weight: they are removed from the name before scoring, and a query made only of them gets no candidate. A query word that matches an age word of a product makes that product's variant the candidate of its family ("doliprane enfant" returns a children's Doliprane first when the registry has one); a typed strength comes first. A brand's age words do not make another family: DOLIPRANE ADULTES and ASPEGIC ENFANTS are variants of DOLIPRANE and ASPEGIC. A product only for newborns or children (an age word of those groups, NOUVEAU-NE or PEDIATRIQUE in its name, words that stay part of the name) ranks after the others when the query names no age: "fer" proposes the iron generic before FER AP-HP NOUVEAU-NE.
- Substances named in the query: the index knows every substance name, and each part of it around brackets ("Acide ascorbique (vitamine C)": "acide ascorbique", "vitamine C"). A candidate that lacks a substance the query names ranks after the candidates that hold it ("efferalgan codeine" never ranks a paracetamol-only EFFERALGAN first), and the relative cutoff is taken from the best candidate that lacks none. A query made only of substance names and joining words (et, avec, plus) finds the products holding them all, at 0.95 when they hold exactly those substances and 0.9 when they hold others too ("paracetamol codeine", "tramadol paracetamol"). `namedSubstances(index, query)` gives these substances to the confirm screen.
- Prefixes: a query is a prefix of a whole name from 3 characters, but a prefix of one word inside a multi-word name (and a fuzzy match on one word of it) only from 4, so that a short common word ("sel") does not reach a product through the start of a longer word.
- Index: every brand, INN, French and English name, local name (with language) of products, substances and plants. A strength in the query ("1 g", "500", "0,5 g") is read apart and not matched as a name.
- Families: one candidate per family (the brand of a branded product without its age words, the sorted substances of a generic, the plant), with the other products of the family as `variants`, strength ascending then French name (the plainest form first: "comprime" before "comprime effervescent secable"), the same order for every brand, for the confirm screen. A product whose name starts with its first brand is a branded product; the brands of a generic are other names of it, so an INN query still lists the generic first. In a family of generics, the product without strength stands for the family when the query gives none (`paracetamol-generic`, with variants `paracetamol-500-generic` and `paracetamol-1000-generic`); a typed strength selects the matching product, and form words typed ("comprime effervescent", "sirop") the product whose name has them; with none typed, the plainest name of the family stands for it ("Dafalgan 500" gives "DAFALGAN 500 mg, comprime").
- Scoring: exact normalised match, then whole word, then prefix, then trigram similarity (Jaccard) combined with Damerau-Levenshtein (optimal string alignment) distance normalised by length; ties broken by a matching strength (a strength written in the name counts for a product without a strength in mg: "aspegic 1000" gives ASPEGIC ADULTES 1000 mg), the form words of the query, a matching age word, a product not only for children, single-substance before combination, entry priority (product brands before substances) and then by id for determinism. Fewer substances named in the query and missing from the product comes before the score. A whole name with a word of 4 letters or more found inside a longer query, its words one after the other and whatever the words around it, is contained in it, at 0.9 ("Advil pour les douleurs", "cytotec de la pharmacie", "mon cytotec", "huile de ricin bio"). A whole name of 6 letters or more spelt within one edit is a near match ("cytotek"). A candidate lacking a substance the query names is kept only when its score is at least that of the best candidate holding them all.
- Output: at most 3 candidates with kind, id, family, variants, display name, detail, matched name and its language, score. Candidates below 0.55, or below 0.8 times the best candidate's score, are dropped; with no candidate left the app offers "Produit introuvable". Each candidate also says how its best name matched (`match`: exact, word, prefix, near, fuzzy, fuzzy-word, substances, contained) and the length of the cleaned query (`queryLength`), so the app can weigh a resemblance (possible matches, section 7.1). A `word` match says when the word alone names none of the products (`weakWord`): `shared` when the names of several families have it (a laboratory, "biogaran"), `inner` when it leads none of the names of the product, coming after its brand word (a qualifier, "chrono" of DEPAKINE CHRONO, "emulgel" of VOLTARENE EMULGEL; a word of a description, "cutanee"); a word after only substance names or a prefix of 3 letters or fewer is the brand ("acnetrait" of ISOTRETINOINE ACNETRAIT, "codion" of Neo-Codion, "profenid" of BI PROFENID LP), and the first word of a name in brackets leads it ("oseille" of "Bissap (oseille de Guinee)"); a test reads every brand word and local name of the pack and finds none `inner`; the score and the order are unchanged (live test round 2, SCRUM-50).
- Never decides a level.
- Evaluation: `eval/resolver/queries.yaml` (query, expected ids, kind of query: brand, inn, misspelling, local-fr, local-wo, voice-like transcription, and noise, a form, a colour, a generic word or a short common word alone that must get no candidate); `pnpm eval:resolver` reports top-1 and top-3 accuracy and p95 latency; the CI fails below the recorded baseline, or on an empty query file, an invalid baseline or a measure that is not a number.
- Target: under 50 ms per query on an entry-level phone (measured in CI on the runner with a 4x CPU slowdown factor applied to the budget: 12 ms on the runner).

A name kept as unrecognised is looked up again in both lists (`app/src/domain/possible-matches.ts`): its first candidate, unless the check already holds that product family or remedy, is added to the evaluation as a possible match (its strength unknown unless the text gives it, and unknown when the text gives another strength than the only one of the product). How clearly the text names it: very strong (a whole name or word, a name contained in the text, a score of 0.9 or more), strong (a score of 0.8 or more, or a near match: "cytotek"), weak otherwise; a prefix of fewer than 6 letters ("Anta"), a spelling close to one word of a longer name ("vitamines") and a whole word that names none of the products alone (`weakWord`, section 6: "biogaran", "dose", "chrono", "cutanee") are weak. After "Non, aucun de ces ...", only a strong or very strong match is kept, and only a very strong one may make the result a danger: a danger found through a strong match is held at "before the next dose" (`cappedEvaluation`; a danger sign is never held down), and its card branches on intake, so the urgency is never lowered when it may already have been taken: "S'il a deja ete pris, allez au poste de sante maintenant et montrez-le. Sinon, montrez-le a la sage-femme ou a l'infirmier avant d'en prendre, pour savoir si c'est bien ce produit." ("ce remede" for a remedy; in accompanied mode "Si elle en a deja pris, elle doit aller au poste de sante maintenant et montrer ce produit. Sinon, ... avant qu'elle en prenne, ..."), with "Voir qui appeler" under it (committee question N-3). Its cards carry the name typed and start with "Ce nom ressemble a ADVIL 400 mg, comprime enrobe (Ibuprofene 400 mg). Si c'est ce produit :" ("ce remede" for a remedy); the summary row says "(ressemble a ADVIL 400 mg ...)" after the level, and so does the danger banner when the danger comes only from resemblances. The banner and the overall level take the highest level, so a name that clearly names a product of the base is not evaluated lower than what the base knows for it. The text itself stays unrecognised, with its grey card, which then starts with "Si ce n'est pas ce produit :" ("ce remede"), and the history keeps the items as checked. Never for an outdated term: the outdated-term step shows instead.
10. Danger signs (reachable in one tap from every screen through the header): the fixed list and the referral circuit with call buttons: the contacts of the phone and the deployment, then always the emergency numbers (built in or the deployment's), locked or not, all before the danger instruction so that a call stays in the first screen whatever its length; then the signs to say and where to go; once the code is given, a way to add or change the contacts in both modes (the contacts screen calls each number too).
11. Knowledge base status and About: version, date (written as on the result: "04/11/2026" in French, "4 November 2026" in English, one format per language on every screen), rule count, out-of-date warning after 90 days ("Base de connaissances ancienne", never "perimee", read as an expired medicine) (also on Home and at the top of every result), intended use (French name "SIAPSO Mere-Enfant" in French), disclaimer, licences, sources (name, licence and version in the language of the screen, with the language of their documents when it is another), repository link. The technical reason of a refused or rolled back base, in English, is labelled "(en anglais)" on a French screen.

Navigation: hash routes, browser back works everywhere, one primary action per screen, the danger signs button always visible (the welcome included) and the quick exit after unlock; before the code, the quick exit also shows on the danger signs and who to call. Settings: the language and the mode are native radios applied by a button that appears once the answer differs (the primary action while the choice waits; "Switch to English" said in English, "Changer de mode"), never by the radio alone, so the arrow keys only move through the answers (WCAG 3.2.2); a choice not applied is forgotten at a lock. No product, profile id or contact id ever goes in an address (`#/confirm/product`, `#/profile/edit`, `#/contacts/edit`): it stays in the history entry's state, in memory.

### 7.2 Storage, offline, i18n

- IndexedDB (`idb`): pack (current and previous), profiles, settings. `navigator.storage.persist()` requested after the welcome, again at each launch until the browser grants it, and when the app is installed; the status screen says whether the data is kept for good or may be evicted when the phone runs short of space.
- Service worker (vite-plugin-pwa, generateSW): precache of the app shell, fonts and pack; update prompt instead of silent reload. Registered with workbox-window (bundled) before the first render, without waiting for the load event; its first install claims the page (`clientsClaim`, no `skipWaiting`). Home, the list of profiles and the status screen say "Prêt sans Internet" once a worker controls the page or is active for it (after a hard reload), and until then say what to do (keep the Internet on, turn it back on, open the app again with it, or open it in Chrome). A first visit offline shows the browser's error page: the handout says to open the app once with the Internet (`docs/testing.md`). Voice files are cached separately (Cache API) only when the user installs the voice pack.
- i18n: typed dictionaries `fr.json` and `en.json`; a test fails if keys differ or a string contains a forbidden word, an em dash or an emoji. The chosen language is stored.
- Budget: app (JS, CSS, fonts, icons) under 1.5 MB uncompressed, checked in CI; pack and voice excluded.

### 7.3 Design and accessibility

- `DESIGN.md` and `app/src/styles/tokens.css` from SCRUM-16 (impeccable `shape`, `critique`, `audit`, `detect`).
- Level colours: red, orange, yellow, grey; never green; each level has a distinct pictogram shape and its name in text.
- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for every text and background pair (tested), 48 px touch targets, 360 px minimum width, readable in sunlight, no gesture-only action, focus visible, screen reader labels.
- From the live test of SCRUM-50 (tested end to end in `app/e2e/layout.spec.ts`): words never break in the middle at 200 percent text on a 360 px phone (the lines that say what to do step down to the reading size, the longest danger instruction of the pack is tested; a reading word still too wide in a narrow result card is hyphenated, never a product name); a number in a product name is held to its unit by a no-break space; on a 320 x 640 phone the code screens keep their primary action in the first screen and the band keeps to two rows, offline too, the danger signs shortcut alone with the way home on its first row, in one place at every width and in every state (on a band of 37.5rem or more, a desktop or a tablet, one row: the name, the offline badge and the quick exit, then the shortcut, which still ends the row in every state, the reading and focus order unchanged; live test round 2, depot-keeper#12. Follow-up, option (b) of the review of #58: put the markup in the order of the row (the name, the danger signs shortcut, then "Quitter") with a reserved invisible slot for the quick exit and the badge in every state, so the focus order and the visual order match on a wide band too while the shortcut keeps one place; not done here, as the slot must hold the longest label of each language and stay out of the accessibility tree); a sticky action never hides the focused element; a Wolof name carries `lang="wo"` and a Latin name `lang="la"`; a status region that has something to say when its screen appears (a wait after a reload, a contact just saved) renders empty, then says it, and a wait is said with the time left; the danger banner's instruction is said once; the page title stays the neutral app name on every screen (`docs/privacy.md`).

## 8. Voice (phase 4)

- Model: `M9and2M/whisper-small-wolof` (MIT), converted to ONNX for Transformers.js with a reproducible script (`speech/convert/`, Python virtual environment, optimum), 8-bit weights. French quality is measured; if the Wolof fine-tune degrades French below a usable level, the French option uses the multilingual Whisper base model (MIT) instead, and the card says so.
- Runtime: Transformers.js in a dedicated Web Worker, WebAssembly backend (WebGPU when available). Audio captured with `getUserMedia`, resampled to 16 kHz mono, kept in memory only, never stored or sent.
- Voice pack: optional download with progress, stored in the Cache API, from a configurable base URL (default: same origin `/voice/`). Keyboard search works without it.
- Flow: hold or tap the microphone, transcribe, pass the text to the resolver, show candidates for confirmation; if no candidate, ask to type or pick from the list, and offer "Je ne trouve pas ce produit" at once (through "Ce nom ressemble à :", as for a typed name); if no name was heard, one message ("Je n'ai pas entendu de nom. Réessayez, ou écrivez le nom."). No level without confirmation. A name the voice heard is labelled "Vous avez dit" on the confirm screen, the check list and the result. The whole list has a filter and a row of letters, and lists the names on the boxes of the generics (Cytotec under C) as entries that open the generic; local names say "Nom local" for Wolof and "Autre nom" for French or English (live test round 2, SCRUM-54).
- Voice screen (live test round 2, SCRUM-54): the language is asked, never chosen for her ("Dans quelle langue allez-vous dire les noms ?", Wolof first with its size), with the warning that another language later means downloading the other voice; offline, the install button is off and says to connect; an installation failure says whether the voice is not available from this server or without the Internet, the files received are damaged, or the download stopped with part of it kept; only a message where part of the voice is kept (a download cut short, one damaged file dropped after the files before it) names "Reprendre l'installation"; none sends her to report it to the health post. A refused microphone is explained in steps for Chrome, or for the installed app (display mode standalone), which has no browser menu.
- Evaluation: `eval/speech/` with a manifest format for consented recordings, a script for word error rate and name accuracy after resolution, and a baseline on the Wolof FLEURS test split (CC BY 4.0). Volunteer recordings are added by the team.

## 9. Phase 6 components

- Signed packs (SCRUM-38): the manifest is signed (Ed25519, detached signature `manifest.sig`); public keys embedded in the app; the private key is never in the repository (GitHub secret for CI builds, offline file for releases). The app verifies before activation, refuses a version lower than the active one, fetches only fragments whose hash changed, keeps the previous pack for rollback.
- Encrypted profiles (SCRUM-39): see section 2 and `docs/threat-model.md`; profiles, history and saved contacts sealed at rest, the situations of a saved profile and the notes on contraception hidden on screen until a tap (the app shows no history screen yet).
- QR transfer (SCRUM-40): profile encrypted with a one-time transfer code (AES-GCM, PBKDF2), payload prefixed `SIAPSO1:` and base64url encoded, size checked against QR capacity; scanning with `BarcodeDetector` when present, otherwise a JavaScript decoder.
- Adverse event reports (SCRUM-41): minimal form (product or remedy, pregnancy term or breastfeeding, event, date, seriousness), no causality assessment, explicit consent, stored locally, exported as JSON and CSV in a provisional format to be confirmed with ARP; never sent automatically and never presented as care.
- API (SCRUM-37): `POST /v1/evaluate` (body: `pack_version`, `context`), `GET /v1/pack`, `GET /v1/health`; API keys in `Authorization: Bearer`, hashed at rest, per-key quotas; OpenAPI 3.1 document; no identifying field accepted (the schema rejects unknown fields); parity test runs every reference case through the API and the library and compares outputs.
- DHIS2 export (SCRUM-43): monthly aggregates per district in `dataValueSets` JSON, with a mapping file for data element and org unit identifiers (no invented identifiers), same suppression rule as open data.
- Open data (SCRUM-35): weekly counts per region (checks, findings per level, share of grey, unrecognised names), field dictionary, suppression of cells of 10 or fewer, test data labelled as such.
- Lighter voice model (SCRUM-42): distillation and quantisation scripts and an evaluation protocol; training needs a GPU and consented data.
- Audit (SCRUM-44): full-history secret and licence scan, report in `docs/audit/`.

## 11. Quality gates

- Unit tests (Vitest) for engine, resolver, pack tools, app logic; component tests with Testing Library.
- Reference cases (`eval/cases/`): the 10 annex cases plus the unknown-product case and term variants, each with the expected level, check ids, rule ids and message ids. Any change in output fails CI; expectations change only by editing the case file in a reviewed pull request.
- Content gates: schema, provenance, forbidden sources, evidence cap, danger rationale, message completeness in both languages, forbidden words.
- End-to-end (Playwright, Chromium, mobile viewport 360 x 800): woman journey, accompanied journey, danger signs from every screen, offline after first load (context offline and reload), language switch, axe accessibility scan with zero serious or critical violations.
- Budgets: app size, pack size, resolver latency.
- CI workflow `ci.yml`: install (frozen lockfile), lint, type check, unit tests with coverage, `pnpm knowledge:check`, resolver evaluation, build, then `git diff --exit-code` (the build must not change a committed file such as `NOTICE`), app budget, end-to-end. Existing `hygiene.yml` stays. All checks must pass before `tools/github/merge-pr.sh` merges: it waits until the four required checks "Text hygiene", "Developer Certificate of Origin", "Build and test" and "End-to-end" are reported, and merges only when each of them passed (a skipped or missing required check blocks the merge, except the Developer Certificate of Origin check of a Dependabot pull request, which `hygiene.yml` skips by design; other checks must pass or be skipped). `tools/github/merge-pr.test.sh` tests the script with a fake `gh` in CI, after `shellcheck`.

## 12. Delivery process

For each ticket: implementer agent on its own branch with test-driven development, signed-off Conventional Commits with `Refs: SCRUM-<n>`; pull request with the template; a specification review and a code quality review by separate agents; fixes; merge with `tools/github/merge-pr.sh` once CI is green; Jira ticket moved to Done with the pull request link.

At the end of each phase, a live test round: several agents each play a real user (Bajenu Gox in accompanied mode using Wolof names, a literate woman alone on a 360 px phone, a midwife creating profiles at an antenatal visit, a pharmacy depot keeper at a sale, a safety auditor who tries to obtain a "safe" or green answer, an accessibility auditor, an airplane-mode tester) and drive the production build with Playwright. Findings are triaged, fixed in follow-up pull requests, and the round is repeated until no blocking finding remains. The Playwright MCP server is configured for the team in `.mcp.json`.

Testing on a phone in airplane mode needs HTTPS or localhost: `docs/testing.md` describes USB port forwarding (Chrome remote debugging, private) and a temporary HTTPS tunnel, then airplane mode after the first load.

## 13. Open questions for the clinical committee

Engineering defaults chosen as the conservative option, to be confirmed or changed by the pharmacist and the midwife of the committee. None of them is clinically validated. Questions 1 to 6 come from the safety review of SCRUM-45.

1. **Term look-ahead of 14 days** (`TERM_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS`, engine). A rule that gives a finding is looked up with the upper end of the term moved on by 14 days, because a course of treatment goes on over the coming days. Is 14 days the right margin, and should it differ per rule (a single dose against a course of several days)?
2. **Findings reached only through the look-ahead.** May such a finding be a danger (castor oil with a uterine scar at 28 weeks gives "allez immediatement")? Is it acceptable to show a rule worded "a partir de 20 semaines" at 18 weeks, or should the message say that the window is reached in the coming days?
3. **Estimated terms near term.** Should an estimated (month) term become outdated on its upper end, or should breastfeeding rules apply as possible instead of blocking (the interim default: possibly past term once the upper end is past 42+0 weeks, blocking only once the lower end is)? Is a month 9 answer (36 to 42 weeks) acceptable at all, or should weeks or the last period date be required near term (the app only suggests them for month 8 or 9)?
4. **How long "Je ne sais pas" holds.** How long does a "Je ne sais pas" answer (to "Enceinte ?" or to the term) stay valid before it is asked again? Interim default: possibly past term after 28 days (`UNKNOWN_HOLDS_DAYS`), blocking after 42 weeks.
5. **After the birth, without breastfeeding.** After "Oui, le bebe est ne" with "Allaite : Non", the app no longer covers her (it offers to delete the profile). Are postpartum women who do not breastfeed out of scope?
6. **Reminders and the look-ahead.** Is it right that the IPTp reminder is not shown at 10 to 11 weeks (reminders and notes do not use the look-ahead, so SP is never asked about before it can be given)?
7. **Unknown baby age read as the youngest band.** Every age-bounded breastfeeding rule applies when the baby's age is not known, when a pregnancy may be past term, and when the phone's clock is set back on a pregnancy profile.
8. **No "possibly past" stage for a last period date.** A last period date gives an exact term, so its lower and upper ends are the same day: it stays "ok" up to 42+0 weeks and blocks from 42+1, with no "A confirmer" stage. Weeks given today are a range of N+0 to N+6; since live test round 2 a term given today is never "A confirmer" (she was seen pregnant that day), so "42 weeks" given today is "A confirmer" from the next day and blocks once its lower end is past 42+0. This is consistent with the rule (possibly past term on the upper end, blocking on the lower end); is it the behaviour the committee wants?
9. **Past 42 weeks, still pregnant (live test round 2).** "Non, toujours enceinte" on a known term past 42 weeks, a last period or weeks that put the term today past 42+0, or "Oui, plus de 9 mois" read the pregnancy as 42 weeks and over (precision estimated) with the advice "Allez au poste de sante aujourd'hui : une grossesse de plus de 42 semaines doit etre vue." and "Voir qui appeler"; the check goes on. Interim defaults: the answer holds on its day, the baby may be born from the next day, and the question is asked again after 7 days (`POST_TERM_HOLDS_DAYS`); a last period or weeks that put the term at 45 weeks or more are refused as a date to look at again. Is "today" the right urgency, should it be a danger level, and are 7 days and 45 weeks the right bounds?
