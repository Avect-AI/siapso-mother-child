# SIAPSO Mother-Child: implementation design

Status: approved for implementation on 2026-11-03 (the team asked for autonomous delivery, choosing the most rigorous option at each decision).
Scope: Jira SCRUM-15 to SCRUM-44. Product context: `PRODUCT.md`. Agent rules: `CLAUDE.md`.

This document is the single technical reference for every ticket. When a ticket and this document disagree, this document wins and the ticket is updated.

French interface sentences are quoted here without accents for brevity. The interface and the knowledge pack always use correct French spelling, with accents (for example "Allez au poste de santé maintenant.").

## 2. Decisions on gaps and contradictions in the dossier

| Topic | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red for medicines | Red (`danger`) is reserved for danger signs. A product rule may use `danger` only with a written `danger-rationale` (immediate vital risk); the starter base used it once: castor oil at term with a uterine scar (reference case 9); the live test round 1 (SCRUM-49) added misoprostol with a uterine scar and misoprostol late in pregnancy, at least as high as castor oil, and the live test round 2 (SCRUM-52) paracetamol from twice the daily maximum and codeine or tramadol while breastfeeding with a baby danger sign ticked now (a rule may require a danger sign), for the committee to confirm. The interface names a product danger "Danger : a montrer maintenant", never "Signe de danger", on the result and on who to call. A product danger card says its instruction and offers who to call, as the banner does. | Arbitration "red = danger signs only", with the explicit committee exception of case 9. |
| ANSM interaction levels | Contre-indication and association deconseillee map to `before-next-dose`; precaution d'emploi and a prendre en compte map to `next-contact`. | Red is reserved; orange is the highest product level. |
| Evidence cap | A finding with evidence D, E or 0 is capped at `next-contact` unless the rule kind is `toxicity`. The pack build fails when a rule declares a level above its cap, so nothing is capped silently; the engine caps again as a second barrier. | Dossier rule, made explicit. |
| Evidence B at orange | Allowed (reference case 6). The "B to D" wording of the level table is descriptive, not a rule. | Only D, E and 0 are capped. |
| Composite evidence ("C/D for toxicity, E for interaction") | One rule per risk, each with one evidence level. | Keeps the cap computable. |
| Woman mode content | Shows all four levels, the level name and pictogram, the risk sentence without numbers, the orientation, the fixed sentences, the knowledge base version and "not clinically validated". Hides the detailed fact, the evidence level and the sources. | Dossier arbitration D1; the woman still sees the version. Confirmed after the live test round 2 (SCRUM-50): the details are written for a professional and the orientation carries the urgency (docs/intended-use.md, "What a result shows in each mode"). |
| Accompanied mode content | Everything in woman mode, plus fact, detailed risk, evidence level with its wording, sources, rule identifier (these five behind one closed "Pourquoi ?" disclosure per card, SCRUM-50), reminders and contraception information. | Dossier; the result stays short for a slow reader (live test round 1). |
| Known product, no rule triggered | Grey level. A product that no rule reads gets the "nothing found" message, a product that a rule reads, when no rule applies to the situation, "nothing applies"; since the live test round 2 (SCRUM-52) both say one grey wording, for a product the app has recognised: "Notre base ne contient rien sur ce produit pour cette situation. Notre base ne dit pas si ce produit peut faire du mal dans cette situation. Montrez ce produit a la sage-femme ou au pharmacien a la prochaine visite." ("Montrez ce produit a la sage-femme ou a l'infirmier a sa prochaine visite." in accompanied mode; never "Montrez cet ecran", gone for a passer-by), the base silent, which says nothing of what others know, a gap and never an all-clear. The version and date of the base are not put inside the sentence: the result says them near the top and at the end (SCRUM-50). | Never implies safety; keeps four levels; never says "nothing found" about a product the base has a rule on (live test round 1); said positively, so a listener who hears only the end never hears "sans risque" (live test round 1, SCRUM-50). |
| Fixed sentences | Every result ends with: "Ne changez rien a votre traitement sans en parler a un professionnel." (in accompanied mode, about her: "Elle ne doit rien changer a son traitement sans en parler a un professionnel.") then the version line, then "Cet outil ne remplace pas un soignant." On a result with a daily-maximum card (C7) or a danger from a product rule, the first line would contradict the card, so it reads "N'en prenez pas plus avant d'en avoir parle a un professionnel." ("Elle ne doit pas en prendre plus avant d'en avoir parle a un professionnel."). | One canonical variant (annex C), with the live test round 2 variants (SCRUM-52, committee question R-9 of `knowledge/README.md`). |
| Wording | Allowed verbs: montrez, dites, parlez-en, demandez, allez, appelez (show, tell, talk, ask, go, call). Not used: "N'arretez rien", "Verifiez", "autorise" (they contain forbidden words). | Consistency with the forbidden list. |
| Forbidden words test | French: sur (the adjective sûr), sans danger, verifie, verifiez, arretez, ne prenez pas, remplacez, diminuez, prenez plutot, autorise, compatible. English: safe, harmless, verified, verify, stop, do not take, replace, reduce, take instead, authorised, authorized, compatible, no risk. Whole-word, accent-aware, case-insensitive, over every pack message and every interface string. "sans risque" is allowed only inside "ne veut pas dire que c'est sans risque" / "does not mean it is risk-free"; the pack and the interface no longer use even that (SCRUM-50), and the app wording test refuses both phrases on the result of every reference case. | Dossier lists, applied to both languages. |
| Orange timing | Default orientation is "avant la prochaine prise" (before the next dose); a rule may give its own orientation, never later than its level: a long-term treatment says "Parlez-en rapidement ... sans rien changer en attendant" (cases 6 and 8 included), never "dans les prochains jours". A danger orientation always says now. `siapso-pack check` refuses a later timing. | Cases 6 and 8; live test round 1 (one urgency everywhere). |
| Weeks | Weeks of amenorrhoea (SA). Windows: `lt12` (0 to 11), `12-19`, `20-29`, `30-plus`. | Clinical convention in Senegal and France. |
| Term input | Last menstrual period date, weeks, months, or unknown. Months and unknown give a range of weeks; the engine triggers every rule whose windows intersect the range (riskiest window per rule) and flags the finding "terme estime" or "terme inconnu". The engine function `rangeFromMonths(m)` covers weeks 4.33 x (m - 1) + 2 to 4.33 x m + 2 (the month in progress). Because "enceinte de m mois" can mean the month in progress or months completed, the app maps a months answer to the union of both readings: from the start of month m to the end of month m + 1 (to 42 for month 9), precision estimated. Weeks, months and unknown answers are stored with the date they were given (`givenOn`) so the term advances between visits (an older record without it is dated by the profile's `updatedAt`); an LMP answer is a date and advances by itself. A whole-week answer stands for that week and its 6 days: "19 weeks" is 19+0 to 19+6, so one day after it was given its upper end reaches 20 (weeks keep precision exact). The weeks answer asks "Ce terme date de quand ?" with "Aujourd'hui" or "Un autre jour" (a date): a new number clears that answer, which is then asked again (always in accompanied mode, and in woman mode on an edited profile; a new profile of her own keeps "Aujourd'hui"), and a day other than today reads back both ("Environ 32 semaines aujourd'hui (26 semaines le 24/09)"). Unknown is the range 0 to 42. The month field takes two digits and refuses anything outside 1 to 9; 10 to 42 is said to look like weeks, with one tap to give it "En semaines"; month 8 or 9 suggests weeks or the last period date instead. Nothing typed is ever cut short. | Solves "riskiest window when it differs per substance". |
| Outdated and possibly past term | `profileTermStatus(profile, today, notBefore)` (app, `domain/term.ts`) is the one place that says where a pregnancy stands, in days of amenorrhoea: not pregnant; ok; possibly past term (not blocking): the upper end of the term is past 42+0 weeks while its lower end is not (for example month 8 or 9 after a few days, weeks 41 or 42), or an unknown-term or "Je ne sais pas" answer is older than 28 days (`UNKNOWN_HOLDS_DAYS`); outdated (blocking): the lower end of the term is past 42+0 weeks, an unknown-term or "Je ne sais pas" answer is older than 42 weeks, or a stored value cannot be read; clock: the phone's date is before a day the app has already seen (the profile's `updatedAt`, its last history entry, the knowledge pack's date) or before the day a term was given, for every kind of term, the last period included. Possibly past term: the check runs with the pregnancy range held at 42 weeks and breastfeeding added with no baby age and no prematurity (every breastfeeding rule applies); the result says "Nous ne savons pas si le bebe est ne : ce resultat tient compte de la grossesse et de l'allaitement." ("si votre bebe" in woman mode) and the profile list says "A confirmer : le bebe est peut-etre ne" with "Mettre a jour" as the way to her profile ("A verifier", first proposed, is not used: no form of "verifier" appears in the interface). Outdated: a required step before any check and as a guard on the result: "Le terme note a passe 42 semaines. Le bebe est-il ne ?" (woman mode: "Votre terme note a passe 42 semaines. Votre bebe est-il ne ?"), with "Oui, le bebe est ne" (the profile opens with the pregnancy set to no and the breastfeeding and baby age questions to answer; "Allaite : Non" then says plainly that the app covers pregnancy and breastfeeding, with the way to delete the profile, and "Enregistrer" is hidden while that answer stands, so no button does nothing) and "Non, toujours enceinte" (the profile opens with the term to give again); both come back to the check. No result is worked out on an outdated profile (`contextFromProfile` throws `OutdatedTermError`); the list says "A mettre a jour". Clock: the term is read as unknown and, the real date being later, breastfeeding is added as for a possibly past term; the result's notice says why and that the baby may be born. Whenever the baby may be born (`babyMayBeBorn`: possibly past term, outdated, clock), the danger signs now screen asks about the signs after the birth and of the baby up front with those of the pregnancy, from the same status and the same days already seen as the check. The danger signs and who to call stay in the header. The result reads the profile and the date again when the app comes back to the front. | A pregnancy profile is kept across visits: after the birth it would hide every breastfeeding rule; a possible birth is not a reason to block a check. |
| Pregnancy not known | "Enceinte ?" offers "Je ne sais pas": stored `pregnant: true, pregnancyUnsure: true` with the day it was said (`pregnancyUnsureOn`, kept while not answered again), read as a pregnancy of unknown term (0 to 42, every window) and dated like an unknown term (possibly past term after 28 days, outdated after 42 weeks); the breastfeeding questions are still asked, and the screens say "Grossesse possible, terme inconnu". | Unknown is not safe. |
| Baby age not known | "Age du bebe" offers "Je ne sais pas": stored `babyAgeUnknown: true` with no age; the engine then applies every age-bounded rule, as for the youngest babies. | Unknown is not safe. |
| Term look-ahead | The engine looks `TERM_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS` (28, it was 14) past the upper end of the term for the rules that give a finding: windows are those of [weeksMin, weeksMax + 4]. It only adds windows; the level stays the highest of the rules that apply. A finding reached only through the look-ahead carries `windowStart`, and its card says "Elle atteindra 20 semaines dans 4 semaines au plus : ce produit demande alors plus de prudence." ("Vous atteindrez" in woman mode; "Elle atteindra peut-etre 20 semaines dans les 4 semaines a venir" for an estimated term). Such a finding is never a danger: a danger rule whose window is still ahead is held at before the next dose, and its card says the orientation of the card's other finding or the level's default, never its "go now" (clinical review of SCRUM-52). Reminders and notes keep the term as it is. The app and the API run the same engine, so they agree. A conservative engineering default (section 13). | A course of treatment goes on over the coming days, and the next visit may be four weeks away (live test round 2, SCRUM-52: ibuprofen at 17 weeks was sent to the next visit though 20 weeks would come first). |
| Profiles on a shared phone | A pseudonym is used once on the phone (trimmed, compared without case or accents) and has at most 40 characters (the limit is said, never cut). The list, the check and the result show the term used in plain words ("Environ 32 semaines", "Entre 6 et 15 semaines (estime)", "Terme inconnu", "Allaite, bebe de 3 mois"), so the right woman and term are seen. "Supprimer ce profil" (profile screen, then a last step) deletes one profile and its history in one transaction, under the code guard as every write of profile data (`requireCode`, NoCodeError from a tab whose code is no longer the one stored); a result kept after its profile was deleted is not written. Woman mode opens her own profile only: a profile made in woman mode is marked `self: true`, one made in accompanied mode `self: false`, and an older unmarked record is hers only when it is the only profile; after she deletes hers, an empty profile of her own opens even when other profiles stay (docs/privacy.md). A save, a confirmation, a deletion or "Voir le resultat" tapped twice runs once. | Live test round 1 (SCRUM-45). |
| Lists of a visit (accompanied mode) | Choosing another woman keeps the unfinished list of the woman left in memory, per profile (items, names typed, lists of unrecognised names, "none of these" answers; not the danger signs, asked again), and gives it back when she is chosen again; her row says "Liste en cours : n produits". The lists waiting are forgotten at every lock of the tab (`forgetOthers`: two minutes idle or hidden, a lock or another code from another tab), the list on screen staying in memory (with the screen held after its own two minutes); every list goes at the quick exit, the erase and a reload; a deleted profile takes its own (`forget`). Never in storage nor on a history entry (docs/privacy.md). | Live test round 2 (SCRUM-50): a relay moving between women in one compound lost each list without a word. |
| Look up without saving (accompanied mode) | "Regarder sans enregistrer" on the list of profiles: the questions of a profile without a pseudonym (pregnant with "Je ne sais pas", term, breastfeeding and baby age, situations), read with the same term rules as a profile (an unknown or uncertain answer takes the riskiest windows); the answers are a profile kept in memory only (`CheckState.quick`), with no history entry and nothing stored. A new one always starts empty. Shown in accompanied mode only, she is let go when another woman or passer-by is looked at, at every lock of the tab (`forgetQuick`; the code then opens the list of profiles, her screen is not held), at any change of mode, at the quick exit, at the erase and at a reload. An outdated term opens her questions again (`updateQuick` keeps her list), never a profile screen. The situations are asked too: a uterine scar makes castor oil at term a danger (reference case 9). | Live test round 2 (SCRUM-50): a depot keeper needed 16 to 20 taps and a stored profile per customer seen once. |
| Breastfeeding | Baby age in weeks and a prematurity flag; rules may bound the age or require prematurity. | Dossier silent on bands. |
| Danger signs | One fixed list for everyone: the dossier list (fever, bleeding, convulsions, severe headache, baby no longer moving), the case signs (very strong abdominal pain; baby sleeps much more than usual, feeds poorly, breathes with difficulty), completed with WHO danger signs cited as fact-only sources. Every item carries its source and stays "not clinically validated" until the committee aligns it with the national list. | Missing a danger sign is the worst failure. |
| Current danger signs in a check | The check flow asks once "Has she one of these signs now?" before the result. The question always opens with nothing ticked, never with an earlier answer (even on Back from the result); a change to the list asks it again; what is ticked goes into the check as it changes, so the browser's Forward to the result shows a sign ticked after Back, and a result with no answer asks the question again. | Cases 2 and 10 need the fever; "now" may have changed since the last answer. |
| Paracetamol maximum (C7) | Optional "units per day" for products that have a maximum-dose rule; the engine sums strength x units per day per substance and compares with the threshold stored in the rule with its source. The message never states a dose. The paracetamol generic has strength variants (`paracetamol-500-generic`, `paracetamol-1000-generic`) in its resolver family, so the confirm screen can offer the strength. With the strength on the box unknown ("Je ne sais pas"), the engine reads the strengths of the product's family taken by the same route (the pack carries the resolver family of each product): the lowest adult one counts as certain (strengths below the substance's `adultMinStrengthMg`, 400 mg for paracetamol, are children's forms, left out unless the family has no other), the highest as possible. A certain excess is the rule's own card; an excess only possible gives "Selon le dosage de la boite, cette quantite peut depasser le maximum par jour." with "Montrez les boites a la sage-femme, a l'infirmier ou au pharmacien avant la prochaine prise.", held at before the next dose (`max-dose-may-exceed`). A daily amount of at least twice the maximum (`paracetamol-overdose`, `fromMgPerDay` 6000, an engineering default) is a danger: "Allez au poste de sante maintenant, meme si tout semble aller bien, et montrez les boites."; possibly toxic (only with the highest strength), it is held at before the next dose with its own message (the rule's `possibleMessage`: "Selon le dosage de la boite, cette quantite peut abimer gravement le foie.", "Montrez les boites au poste de sante aujourd'hui, meme si tout semble aller bien.", with who to call), never left out behind a certain daily-maximum card. When neither the product nor its family gives a strength, the engine adds the note `max-dose-not-computable` ("La quantite totale ne peut pas etre calculee sans le dosage. Montrez les boites a la sage-femme, a l'infirmier ou au pharmacien avant la prochaine prise."), shown in both modes, once, never a level. | MVP check without giving doses; the default path (typing "paracetamol") must not silently skip the check; an unknown strength is never handled less cautiously than a known one (live test round 2, SCRUM-52). |
| Preventive calendar (C10) | Reminders ("demandez si vous avez recu...") are separate from levels and shown in accompanied mode only; the IPTp reminder is never shown to a woman living with HIV on cotrimoxazole: neither when the condition `hiv-cotrimoxazole` is entered nor when a checked item contains sulfamethoxazole (rule key `unless-with`, reference case 15). | Dossier; a woman may enter her cotrimoxazole as a medicine without the condition. |
| Contraception (C9) | General information notes only (formulation 8), accompanied mode only, never a verdict. | Decree 2023-2419. |
| Codes | Every profile has a 4 to 6 digit code. A code among the first ones tried (one digit repeated, digits in a row up or down, a year from 1950 to 2030) gets a plain warning ("Ce code est facile a deviner", not to use a year of birth) and a second yes ("Garder ce code"), never a refusal (live test round 2, SCRUM-50). Five wrong attempts lock for 30 seconds, doubling each time, up to 30 minutes; the wait is shown in minutes and seconds ("Attendez 1 min 59 s") and said in words to a screen reader ("1 minute 59 secondes"), and the keypad key that takes the last digit off says "Corriger", never the word EFFACER of the forgotten-code erase. Auto-lock after 2 minutes without interaction. Quick exit locks immediately and shows a neutral screen. The quick exit, a lock asked for and the forgotten-code erase reach every tab of the app (a BroadcastChannel, plus an epoch of random marks in local storage (and its storage event) and in IndexedDB, which each tab checks with the stored code on focus, visibilitychange and pageshow, for a frozen or cached tab); every tab locks and forgets its held screen, or returns to the welcome after the erase. An auto-lock stays in its own tab. A code is stored only when none is (a tab left on the welcome asks for the stored code instead of making a second one, and says a code exists), and opens the app only once storage shows it stored; a welcome cut short after the code (onboarded false, a code stored) shows the code screen, then asks the mode; profile, history and contact writes, and the mode and welcome in the settings, go through only with the code the tab opened (its verifier's salt and hash, compared in the write's transaction). A tab follows an erase or a new code only once the stored code shows it. Danger signs ticked without the code are forgotten after 2 minutes without interaction or hidden, at every lock and at a reload, and the quick exit is offered on the danger signs and who to call before the code. What screens leave on history entries stays in memory; the entry holds only marks. The answers of a profile or contact form not saved yet are kept in memory only (never stored) across an auto-lock and given back to the same screen after the code, said not saved; the quick exit, a lock from another tab, the erase, another code or leaving the screen drop them; while they are not saved, a band warns 30 s before the auto-lock, and a touch puts it off (WCAG 2.2.1); a passer-by's answers are warned about too, then go with her at the lock. Details: `docs/privacy.md`. | Shared phones; mobile money users know 4-digit codes. Live test round 1 (SCRUM-48): a second tab stayed open after "Quitter" and could write a profile back after the erase. |
| App name on the home screen | Build-time setting, default "Carnet" (neutral) with a neutral icon. | Dossier asks for a neutral name. |
| Phone numbers | No personal number in the repository: only the public national emergency numbers of Senegal (SAMU 1515, fire brigade 18) in `app/src/config/emergency.ts`, shown on every who to call screen with "Allez au poste de sante ou au centre de sante le plus proche."; the deployment file may replace the numbers and adds its circuit text under that line; the team confirms them before a pilot. Referral contacts are entered on the phone behind the code, in both modes, or provided by a deployment file `referral.json` that is not committed (an example file is). The app page answered for that file (HTML) is read as no file, a valid file loads whatever its content type, the service worker never answers `/config/` with the app page, and a production build without the file, with invalid JSON or with entries the phones would leave out prints a loud warning. | Tickets SCRUM-26 and SCRUM-46: a danger must always lead to a number to call. |
| Open data threshold | A cell is published only when its count is greater than 10. | Literal reading of "au-dela de 10", the stricter option. |
| Server stack | One Node.js service in TypeScript that imports the same engine package. No FastAPI layer. | Parity by construction, one runtime, fewer moving parts. The dossier's FastAPI gateway can be added in front later without changing the engine. |
| Signing | Ed25519 with `@noble/ed25519` (works on older Android browsers where WebCrypto Ed25519 is missing). | Dossier lists noble-ed25519. |
| Profile encryption | One PBKDF2-SHA-256 derivation per unlock (600 000 iterations, random 16-byte salt), split with HKDF-SHA-256 into the stored verifier check and a non-extractable wrapping key kept in memory only while open. Envelope encryption: every profile, history entry and the saved contacts are sealed with AES-GCM under their own random 256-bit data key, wrapped by that key (random 96-bit IVs, additional data binding the envelope version, the store and the record id); the phone's one code (hers in woman mode, the accompanying person's in accompanied mode) opens every profile of the phone. The key is forgotten at every lock, the quick exit, a lock from another tab, the erase and another code. The first right code after the update seals what was kept in clear and stores a new verifier in one transaction. The situations of a saved profile, a note on contraception and the note of an HIV-related situation are shown at a tap only. The threat model (casual access, a lost phone or storage read without the code; not a forensic attacker who tries every code offline) is in `docs/threat-model.md`. | Ticket SCRUM-39. A key derived from the old verifier (the PBKDF2 output itself, stored) would be readable from storage; HKDF keeps one derivation per unlock. History and contacts hold names, levels, typed text and numbers: sealed too. |
| Interface languages | French and English. Wolof is supported in names and voice input, not as interface text. | Team decision; Wolof written interface undecided. |
| A name typed as unrecognised that the base knows (live test round of 2026-11-05) | "Je ne trouve pas" asks first when the text looks like products or remedies of either list; a name kept as typed is evaluated again as its best candidate of both lists, shown as a possible match, while the text stays unknown. | Free text must never give a lower result than the base knows (castor oil typed among the medicines, Advil typed as its box form). Models propose, rules decide: the engine sets the level of the possible match. |

## 4. Knowledge model

### 4.1 Authoring format (YAML, validated by JSON Schema 2020-12)

Registry:

- `substances.yaml`: `id` (INN, kebab-case), `names` (`fr`, `en`), `classes` (class ids).
- `classes.yaml`: `id`, `names`, `excludes` (required, possibly empty: substance ids left out of the class, for example low-dose aspirin rules later).
- `products/<id>.yaml`: `id`, `names` (`fr`, `en`), `brands` (list: the brand of a branded product, or the brands people use for a generic, such as Coartem for artemether-lumefantrine), `local-names` (list of `text`, `lang` in `fr|wo|en`, `checked` boolean: validated by a speaker), `components` (at least one; `substance`, `strength-mg` optional), `form`, `route`, `source` (`bdpm` with CIS code, or `demo-manual`), `demo` boolean. `registry/products/bdpm.yaml` is a generated list of products; the other names people use for a brand ("Doliprane codeine" for CODOLIPRANE) are given to its products by the import (`LOCAL_NAMES`), as unchecked French local names.
- `plants/<id>.yaml`: `id`, `latin-name`, `names`, `local-names`, `parts`, `evidence` (overall), `message` (default message id for the grey state), `sources`.

Knowledge:

- `rules/<id>.yaml`: `id`, `kind` (`pregnancy`, `breastfeeding`, `interaction`, `herb-drug`, `toxicity`, `duplicate`, `max-dose`, `condition`, `reminder`, `contraception-info`), `when` (match, below), `level`, `evidence`, optional `danger-rationale`, `message` (message id), `sources` (list of `source` id from `sources.yaml`, `locator` (record title), `url`, `accessed` date, optional `quote`), `review` (`status` `draft|validated`, `reviewers` list of name and role, `reviewed-on`). A rule has no `licence` field: every content file is CC BY 4.0 through its first line `# SPDX-License-Identifier: CC-BY-4.0`.
- `when` keys (kebab-case, mapped to the engine `RuleWhen` of section 5.1): `subject` (`{ substance }`, `{ class }` or `{ plant }`, exactly one key; required except for `reminder`), `requires` (non-empty any-of list of `{ with: <subject> }`, another item, or `{ condition: <id> }`; an `interaction` or `herb-drug` rule needs a `with`), `unless-conditions` (the rule does not apply when one of these conditions is present), `unless-with` (non-empty list of subjects: the rule does not apply when any item matches one), `strength-above-mg` (substance subject only, not on `max-dose` or `duplicate`), `max-mg-per-day` (required for `max-dose`, only there), `pregnancy-windows` (non-empty list of window ids), `breastfeeding` (`baby-age-weeks-max`, `premature`).
- `messages/<id>.yaml`: per language `fr` and `en`: `fact`, `risk` (no numbers), optional `risk-detail` (may hold numbers, accompanied mode), optional `orientation` (overrides the level default).
- `danger-signs.yaml`: `id`, `group` (`pregnancy`, `postpartum`, `baby`), `label` (`fr`, `en`), `sources`.
- `sources.yaml`: `allowed` (id, name, publisher, licence, version or download date, url, notice text), `fact-only` (WHO documents: may support a fact, never copied text), `forbidden` (DDInter, LEDIAM, e-lactancia, CRAT, Theriaque, Vidal, full DrugBank: with the reason).
- `pack.yaml`: `version` (semver), `date` (ISO date of the content freeze), `clinically-validated: false`.

Condition ids: `hiv-cotrimoxazole`, `tuberculosis-treatment`, `epilepsy-treatment`, `uterine-scar`, `renal-failure`, `hepatic-failure`, `sickle-cell`.

### 4.2 Build and runtime pack

`pnpm knowledge:build` validates every file, checks provenance (every rule cites at least one `allowed` source, no `forbidden` id or domain appears anywhere), checks the evidence cap and the danger rationale, checks message ids and forbidden words, then writes `app/public/pack/`:

- `manifest.json`: `schema` (integer), `version`, `date`, `clinicallyValidated`, `counts`, `fragments` (path, sha256, bytes);
- fragments, in this order (`PACK_FRAGMENTS` of `@siapso/pack-tools`; a test of the app checks that its loader asks for exactly these): `substances.json`, `classes.json`, `products.json`, `plants.json`, `rules.json`, `danger-signs.json`, `sources.json`, `messages.fr.json`, `messages.en.json`;
- `pack.json`: the whole pack in one file, for tools and tests (not listed in the manifest).

There is no resolver index file: the app builds the resolver index on the device from the products, plants and substances of the pack.

The runtime format is typed by `@siapso/engine` (`Pack`); the build script is typed against it, but only in part. Type-checked: the products and plants (each field is mapped explicitly), the metadata (`version`, `date`, `clinicallyValidated`), the sources, and the keys of a rule `when` (a test compares the keys of the rule schema with `RULE_WHEN_KEYS`, which the engine types by `RuleWhen`). Checked at runtime only, by `parsePack` on the built pack (the build fails, naming the file): the messages, the top level of a rule (these are cast to `Rule`, `Message` and `DangerSign` after the camelCase conversion), and the danger signs. So a change to one of those engine types needs the matching schema and tests to be updated by hand. The build is deterministic (no clock: version and date come from `pack.yaml`); building twice gives identical bytes. Budget: under 3 MB gzip. The build also generates `NOTICE` from `sources.yaml`.

## 5. Engine

### 5.1 Contract

```ts
type Level = 'danger' | 'before-next-dose' | 'next-contact' | 'unknown';
type Evidence = 'A' | 'B' | 'C' | 'D' | 'E' | '0';
type WindowId = 'lt12' | '12-19' | '20-29' | '30-plus';
type TermPrecision = 'exact' | 'estimated' | 'unknown';

interface EvaluationContext {
  pregnancy?: { weeksMin: number; weeksMax: number; precision: TermPrecision };
  breastfeeding?: { babyAgeWeeks?: number; premature?: boolean };
  conditions: ConditionId[];
  items: CheckedItem[];      // confirmed by the user; long-term treatments included with longTerm: true
  signs: DangerSignId[];     // danger signs present now
}
type CheckedItem =
  | { ref: { kind: 'product' | 'plant'; id: string }; unitsPerDay?: number; longTerm?: boolean; strengthUnknown?: boolean } // strengthUnknown: the strength on the box is not known, component strengths read as unknown, a daily maximum reads the strengths of the product's family
  | { ref: { kind: 'unrecognised'; text: string } };

interface Finding {
  level: Level;
  check: CheckId;            // C1 to C12
  ruleId?: string;
  evidence?: Evidence;
  items: number[];           // indexes into context.items
  message: string;           // message id
  term?: TermPrecision;      // set when a pregnancy window matched on a range
  windowStart?: number;      // set when reached only through the term look-ahead: the week its window starts
  capped: boolean;
  sources: SourceRef[];
}
interface Evaluation {
  level: Level;              // highest finding level
  findings: Finding[];       // sorted: level (danger first), then check, then rule id
  reminders: Reminder[];     // C10, accompanied mode only
  notes: Note[];             // C9 (kind 'contraception', accompanied mode only) and max-dose-not-computable (kind 'dose', both modes)
  pack: { version: string; date: string; clinicallyValidated: boolean };
}
interface Reminder { ruleId: string; message: string; sources: SourceRef[] }
interface Note extends Reminder { kind: 'contraception' | 'dose' }  // the app filters on kind
function evaluate(pack: Pack, context: EvaluationContext): Evaluation;

type Subject = { substance: string } | { class: string } | { plant: string };
interface RuleWhen {
  subject?: Subject;                // required for every kind except reminder
  requires?: ({ with: Subject } | { condition: ConditionId } | { sign: string })[]; // any-of; sign: a danger sign present now
  unlessConditions?: ConditionId[]; // the rule does not apply when one is present
  unlessWith?: Subject[];           // reminder and contraception-info only: the rule does not apply when any item matches one
  pregnancyWindows?: WindowId[];
  breastfeeding?: { babyAgeWeeksMax?: number; premature?: boolean };
  maxMgPerDay?: number;             // max-dose only: above it
  fromMgPerDay?: number;            // max-dose only, instead: from it, the amount included
  strengthAboveMg?: number;         // substance subject only
}
```

Level order: `danger` > `before-next-dose` > `next-contact` > `unknown`.

### 5.2 Checks

| Check | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| C1 pregnancy | Applies only to a pregnancy context; rule windows (all windows when none are listed) intersect the windows covered by the context range, its upper end moved on by `TERM_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS` (28 days); a finding reached only that way carries `windowStart` and is held at before-next-dose (never a danger for a window still ahead). The look-ahead applies to every rule that gives a finding (windows are listed on C1 and C5 rules today); reminders (C10) and notes (C9) use the range as it is. |
| C2 breastfeeding | Applies only to a breastfeeding context, within its age bound and prematurity requirement. |
| C3 interaction | Subject and `with` both present among distinct items; a rule without a `with` partner is rejected. |
| C4 herb-drug | Same as C3 with a plant subject and a medicine partner. |
| C5 toxicity | Subject present, context conditions met; not capped by evidence. |
| C6 duplicate | Two or more distinct items share a substance (or a class) that has a duplicate rule. |
| C7 maximum dose | The sum over items of strength-mg x units-per-day for the substance is greater than `maxMgPerDay`, or at least `fromMgPerDay`, even if some items lack a unit count or a strength (a known partial sum above the threshold is a certain excess). An item of unknown strength counts the lowest strength of its family (same route) in the certain sum and the highest in the possible sum: a certain excess gives the rule's finding, an excess only possible a finding held at before-next-dose with the rule's `possibleMessage` or the builtin `max-dose-may-exceed` (left out when a certain C7 finding of at least its level lists its items, never for a danger rule held). The certain sum leaves out the family strengths below the substance's `adultMinStrengthMg`. When the rule could apply and an item holding the substance has units per day but no strength in it or its family, a note `max-dose-not-computable` (kind `dose`) is added (one per rule; the app says one text once), never a level; it is not added when a C7 finding already lists its items (the finding is enough, and the note would contradict it). |
| C8 condition | Subject present and one of the conditions (or the `with` item) present. |
| C9 contraception | Produces a note (kind `contraception`), never a level. |
| C10 calendar | Produces a reminder, never a level. |
| C11 danger signs | Each present sign gives a `danger` finding with the standard danger message. |
| C12 not found | Each unrecognised item gives an `unknown` finding with the "not recognised" message. |

A product or plant with no finding gives an `unknown` finding: plants use their own grey message (evidence 0 wording); products use "nothing applies" (`nothing-applies`) when a rule of the pack that gives findings reads them (as its subject, whatever the strength threshold, or as a partner; not a reminder or a contraception note), else "nothing found". A plant with evidence 0 and no rule always gives grey. The app says, in both modes and before the cards, each situation of the profile that no rule of the pack reads.

### 5.3 Guarantees (tested)

- An unrecognised item always yields `unknown`, whatever else is present.
- No input yields a level outside the four levels; there is no "safe" output.
- Same inputs, same output (pure, deterministic order).
- Unknown term triggers every pregnancy rule for the substance.
- The term look-ahead never removes a window that applies today (`engine/test/lookahead.test.ts`).
- Invalid numbers in the context (non-finite or negative baby age, non-positive units per day) raise an error instead of silently lowering a level; the app validates input before calling the engine.
- Evidence D, E, 0 never exceeds `next-contact` except `toxicity`.
- Coverage of `engine/` at least 90 percent (lines, branches, functions, statements).

## 6. Resolver

- Normalisation: lowercase, NFD with diacritics removed, every character that is not a letter or a digit (apostrophes, hyphens) to a space, collapse spaces (`plainText`, the text as written, which sorts names and files them by first letter), then per word of two letters or more: `ph` to `f`, `ck` and `qu` to `k` (quinqueliba, kinkeliba; nivaquine, nivakine), `c` before `e`, `i` or `y` to `s` and any other `c` but `ch` to `k`, a silent `h` dropped (at the start of a word, and after a consonant other than `c`, `s`, `p` and `k`), `y` to `i`, `ou` to `u`, a doubled letter to one (vowels included, digits kept), a trailing `e` dropped in words of more than 4 letters (so `-que` reads as `-k`). A short query (6 letters or fewer as matched) reaches a name it does not equal (prefix, near, fuzzy or fuzzy-word) only when the match also holds with both spelt as written (`lightCleaned`): "raisin", "résine" and the first name "Coura" reach neither castor oil nor CURACNE. A name written as it sounds is then the name itself (live test round 2, SCRUM-51: "sitotek", "cytotek" and "cytoteck" are Cytotec, "huile de rissin", "huille de rissin" and "uile de risin" are castor oil, "kodeyin" is codeine); over the repository registry no two whole names of different families read alike (`resolver/README.md` lists the words that do). `kh` is not mapped to `x` nor to `k`: it would change the meaning of Wolof names (dakhar stays dakhar).
- Stop-words: the words of pharmaceutical forms and packaging, the generic words medicament and emplatre, and the generic words of remedies (remede, plante, racine, ecorce, graine, tisane) (comprime, pellicule, secable, effervescent, orodispersible, gelule, capsule, sirop, solution, suspension, buvable, poudre, sachet, granules, creme, pommade, gel, collyre, suppositoire, injectable, ampoule, flacon, medicament, medicaments, medicamenteux, emplatre, emplatres, and English tablet, capsule, syrup, cream and the like, accented or not) are removed from indexed names and from the query; a query made only of stop-words gets no candidate ("racine" no longer reaches "Huile de ricin"). Carrier words name nothing either (huile, feuille, mousse, infusion, decoction, jus) and neither does "generique": with a linking word left alone at either end removed, "huile de nim" is neem and "huile de coco" reaches nothing. Box abbreviations of forms (cp, cpr, comp, eff, gel, sach, susp, sol, buv, supp, LP and the like) are stop-words too, read as the forms they stand for when they choose a form ("Efferalgan 1g cpr eff" gives the effervescent EFFERALGAN 1000 mg).
- Salt words: the salt or hydrate a box names next to the substance (sodium, sodique, potassique, chlorhydrate, hyclate, phosphate, sulfate, fumarate, maleate, hemihydrate and the like) are removed from names and queries with the "de" or "d'" that links them, so "valproate de sodium", "valproate sodium" and "chlorhydrate de tramadol" are read as "valproate" and "tramadol". A salt word is kept when it is the whole query ("sodium") or a substance of the registry ("calcium" if the registry had it); magnesium is a salt word ("valproate de magnesium"), and the valproate generic is also named "divalproate de sodium".
- Age words (adulte, enfant, nourrisson and their plurals, and English adult, child, infant) are not stop-words but carry no match weight: they are removed from the name before scoring, and a query made only of them gets no candidate. A query word that matches an age word of a product makes that product's variant the candidate of its family ("doliprane enfant" returns a children's Doliprane first when the registry has one); a typed strength comes first. A brand's age words do not make another family: DOLIPRANE ADULTES and ASPEGIC ENFANTS are variants of DOLIPRANE and ASPEGIC. A product only for newborns or children (an age word of those groups, NOUVEAU-NE or PEDIATRIQUE in its name, words that stay part of the name) ranks after the others when the query names no age: "fer" proposes the iron generic before FER AP-HP NOUVEAU-NE.
- Substances named in the query: the index knows every substance name, and each part of it around brackets ("Acide ascorbique (vitamine C)": "acide ascorbique", "vitamine C"). A candidate that lacks a substance the query names ranks after the candidates that hold it ("efferalgan codeine" never ranks a paracetamol-only EFFERALGAN first), and the relative cutoff is taken from the best candidate that lacks none. A query made only of substance names and joining words (et, avec, plus) finds the products holding them all, at 0.95 when they hold exactly those substances and 0.9 when they hold others too ("paracetamol codeine", "tramadol paracetamol"). `namedSubstances(index, query)` gives these substances to the confirm screen. A brand named in the query implies the substances every product of that brand holds ("ADVIL": ibuprofen), found as its words one after the other, the longest brand first (a brand with no word of 4 letters or more only as the whole query); a candidate lacking one ranks after the ones that hold them all, on the same terms ("Advil codeine" proposes ANTARENE CODEINE first; live test round 2, SCRUM-51). `queryMentions(index, query)` gives the brands and substances a query names, each with its substances.
- Prefixes: a query is a prefix of a whole name from 3 characters, but a prefix of one word inside a multi-word name (and a fuzzy match on one word of it) only from 4, so that a short common word ("sel") does not reach a product through the start of a longer word.
- Index: every brand, INN, French and English name, local name (with language) of products, substances and plants. A strength in the query ("1 g", "500", "0,5 g") is read apart and not matched as a name.
- Families: one candidate per family (the brand of a branded product without its age words, the sorted substances of a generic, the plant), with the other products of the family as `variants`, strength ascending then French name (the plainest form first: "comprime" before "comprime effervescent secable"), the same order for every brand, for the confirm screen. A product whose name starts with its first brand is a branded product; the brands of a generic are other names of it, so an INN query still lists the generic first. In a family of generics, the product without strength stands for the family when the query gives none (`paracetamol-generic`, with variants `paracetamol-500-generic` and `paracetamol-1000-generic`); a typed strength selects the matching product, and form words typed ("comprime effervescent", "sirop") the product whose name has them; with none typed, the plainest name of the family stands for it ("Dafalgan 500" gives "DAFALGAN 500 mg, comprime").
- Scoring: exact normalised match, then whole word, then prefix, then trigram similarity (Jaccard) combined with Damerau-Levenshtein (optimal string alignment) distance normalised by length; ties broken by a matching strength (a strength written in the name counts for a product without a strength in mg: "aspegic 1000" gives ASPEGIC ADULTES 1000 mg), the form words of the query, a matching age word, a product not only for children, single-substance before combination, entry priority (product brands before substances) and then by id for determinism. Fewer substances named in the query and missing from the product comes before the score. A whole name with a word of 4 letters or more found inside a longer query, its words one after the other and whatever the words around it, is contained in it, at 0.9 ("Advil pour les douleurs", "cytotec de la pharmacie", "mon cytotec", "huile de ricin bio"). A whole name of 6 letters or more spelt within one edit is a near match ("misopristol"); a name written as it sounds is the name itself ("sitotek", "cytotek" and "cytoteck" are Cytotec, "huile de rissin" is castor oil, "kodeyin" is codeine: see the normalisation). A candidate lacking a substance the query names is kept only when its score is at least that of the best candidate holding them all.
- Output: at most 3 candidates with kind, id, family, variants, display name, detail, matched name and its language, score. Candidates below 0.55, or below 0.8 times the best candidate's score, are dropped; with no candidate left the app offers "Produit introuvable". Each candidate also says how its best name matched (`match`: exact, word, prefix, near, fuzzy, fuzzy-word, substances, contained) and the length of the cleaned query (`queryLength`), so the app can weigh a resemblance (possible matches, section 7.1). A `word` match says when the word alone names none of the products (`weakWord`): `shared` when the names of several families have it (a laboratory, "biogaran"), `inner` when it leads none of the names of the product, coming after its brand word (a qualifier, "chrono" of DEPAKINE CHRONO, "emulgel" of VOLTARENE EMULGEL; a word of a description, "cutanee"); a word after only substance names or a prefix of 3 letters or fewer is the brand ("acnetrait" of ISOTRETINOINE ACNETRAIT, "codion" of Neo-Codion, "profenid" of BI PROFENID LP), each word of a substance name of several words names it ("mefenamique" of "Acide mefenamique", SCRUM-51), and the first word of a name in brackets leads it ("oseille" of "Bissap (oseille de Guinee)"); a test reads every brand word and local name of the pack and finds none `inner`; the score and the order are unchanged (live test round 2, SCRUM-50).
- Products named inside a sentence (SCRUM-61, team feedback of 2026-11-05): `productsInSentence(index, sentence)` reads a voice transcript or a typed sentence with rules on the closed list of names of the base, through `search` (no generative language model: too heavy offline on low-end phones, and it can invent names; the reading is deterministic, explainable and tested). The words are normalised as names are (`plainText` reads the Wolof ñ as "ny" and ŋ as "ng"), the French and Wolof filler words left out (`FILLER_WORDS`: function words, verbs of taking, words of the context, numbers and answers; none is a whole name of the base), and every run of 1 to 4 other words (across de, d', du, des inside a name; across et, ak, avec only for a whole name: "fer et acide folique") is searched in both lists and kept when its first candidate is strong by the safety net's rules (`matchStrength`, moved to the resolver); the runs are chosen best first, the longest at an equal score, without overlap ("huile de ricin" one remedy, "doliprane ak nivaquine" two products, "doliprane codéine" the product bearing both). A word of 6 letters or more that names nothing clearly is read once more with one Wolof spelling undone (a vowel inside a French cluster, a final vowel, r and l, b and p, ks for x, j for a soft g, s for z between vowels), then strong at most. A common word of a name is never read alone ("noix", "amer"), a name that is also a common word is offered unticked ("goro"), and a strength said after a name is read with it. At an equal score, a candidate says the clearest way one of its names matched (a near spelling of the brand before a close word of its French name). For what the voice heard only, 2 or 3 words outside the names found clearly are also read joined and compared sound for sound (`phoneticKey`) with the whole names of the base, an article the voice moved included ("King Kyliba" kinkéliba, "la moxiceline" amoxicillin, "Libu profène" ibuprofen, which then takes the place of a single word read only as the start of another name): such a name is strong at most and never ticked, the screen saying "À confirmer : j'ai entendu « King Kyliba »". Measured with `pnpm eval:sentences` (section 11) and on 80 utterances of Windows voices (`docs/voice.md`).
- Never decides a level.
- Evaluation: `eval/resolver/queries.yaml` (query, expected ids, kind of query: brand, inn, misspelling, local-fr, local-wo, voice-like transcription, and noise, a form, a colour, a generic word or a short common word alone that must get no candidate); `pnpm eval:resolver` reports top-1 and top-3 accuracy and p95 latency; the CI fails below the recorded baseline, or on an empty query file, an invalid baseline or a measure that is not a number.
- Target: under 50 ms per query on an entry-level phone (measured in CI on the runner with a 4x CPU slowdown factor applied to the budget: 12 ms on the runner).

### 7.2 Storage, offline, i18n

- IndexedDB (`idb`): pack (current and previous), profiles, settings. `navigator.storage.persist()` requested after the welcome, again at each launch until the browser grants it, and when the app is installed; the status screen says whether the data is kept for good or may be evicted when the phone runs short of space.
- Service worker (vite-plugin-pwa, generateSW): precache of the app shell, fonts and pack; update prompt instead of silent reload. Registered with workbox-window (bundled) before the first render, without waiting for the load event; its first install claims the page (`clientsClaim`, no `skipWaiting`). A first visit offline shows the browser's error page: the handout says to open the app once with the Internet (`docs/testing.md`). Voice files are cached separately (Cache API) only when the user installs the voice pack.

## 8. Voice (phase 4)

- Model: `M9and2M/whisper-small-wolof` (MIT), converted to ONNX for Transformers.js with a reproducible script (`speech/convert/`, Python virtual environment, optimum), 8-bit weights. French quality is measured; if the Wolof fine-tune degrades French below a usable level, the French option uses the multilingual Whisper base model (MIT) instead, and the card says so.
- Runtime: Transformers.js in a dedicated Web Worker, WebAssembly backend (WebGPU when available). Audio captured with `getUserMedia`, resampled to 16 kHz mono, kept in memory only, never stored or sent. The worker can give the French decoder the common names of the base as words said before the speech (`TranscribeOptions.context`, SCRUM-61); measured, it writes far more names right without making FLEURS worse, but heard "kinkéliba" as "quinine" three times, so it stays behind a flag, off (`VOICE_NAMES_PROMPT_ON`), until real voices are measured with it (`docs/voice.md`).
- Voice pack: optional download with progress, stored in the Cache API, from a configurable base URL (default: same origin `/voice/`). Keyboard search works without it.
- Flow: hold or tap the microphone, transcribe, pass the text to the resolver, show candidates for confirmation; a sentence with no candidate as a whole, or that names several products, shows the products found inside it to tick, each then confirmed (section 7.1, screens 6 and 7; SCRUM-61); if no candidate, ask to type or pick from the list, and offer "Je ne trouve pas ce produit" at once (through "Ce nom ressemble à :", as for a typed name); if no name was heard, one message ("Je n'ai pas entendu de nom. Réessayez, ou écrivez le nom."). No level without confirmation. A name the voice heard is labelled "Vous avez dit" on the confirm screen, the check list and the result. The whole list has a filter and a row of letters, and lists the names on the boxes of the generics (Cytotec under C) as entries that open the generic; local names say "Nom local" for Wolof and "Autre nom" for French or English (live test round 2, SCRUM-54).
- Voice screen (live test round 2, SCRUM-54): the language is asked, never chosen for her ("Dans quelle langue allez-vous dire les noms ?", Wolof first with its size), with the warning that another language later means downloading the other voice; offline, the install button is off and says to connect; an installation failure says whether the voice is not available from this server or without the Internet, the files received are damaged, or the download stopped with part of it kept; only a message where part of the voice is kept (a download cut short, one damaged file dropped after the files before it) names "Reprendre l'installation"; none sends her to report it to the health post. A refused microphone is explained in steps for Chrome, or for the installed app (display mode standalone), which has no browser menu.
- Evaluation: `eval/speech/` with a manifest format for consented recordings, a script for word error rate and name accuracy after resolution, and a baseline on the Wolof FLEURS test split (CC BY 4.0). Volunteer recordings are added by the team. Products named inside a sentence: `eval/speech/sentences.yaml` (at least 150 invented sentences in French, Wolof and mixed, with the products each names, misspellings, several products and sentences with none) and `pnpm eval:sentences`, which reports precision, recall and false products per language, and false products on the FLEURS transcripts of the speech evaluation; CI fails below the bars of `eval/speech/sentences-baseline.json` (`eval/speech/README.md` says why they are where they are).

## 9. Phase 6 components

- Signed packs (SCRUM-38): the manifest is signed (Ed25519, detached signature `manifest.sig`); public keys embedded in the app; the private key is never in the repository (GitHub secret for CI builds, offline file for releases). The app verifies before activation, refuses a version lower than the active one, fetches only fragments whose hash changed, keeps the previous pack for rollback.
- Encrypted profiles (SCRUM-39): see section 2 and `docs/threat-model.md`; profiles, history and saved contacts sealed at rest, the situations of a saved profile and the notes on contraception hidden on screen until a tap (the app shows no history screen yet).
- QR transfer (SCRUM-40): profile encrypted with a one-time transfer code (AES-GCM, PBKDF2), payload prefixed `SIAPSO1:` and base64url encoded, size checked against QR capacity; scanning with `BarcodeDetector` when present, otherwise a JavaScript decoder.
- Adverse event reports (SCRUM-41): minimal form (product or remedy, pregnancy term or breastfeeding, event, date, seriousness), no causality assessment, explicit consent, stored locally, exported as JSON and CSV in a provisional format to be confirmed with ARP; never sent automatically and never presented as care.
- API (SCRUM-37): `POST /v1/evaluate` (body: `pack_version`, `context`), `GET /v1/pack`, `GET /v1/health`; API keys in `Authorization: Bearer`, hashed at rest, per-key quotas; OpenAPI 3.1 document; no identifying field accepted (the schema rejects unknown fields); parity test runs every reference case through the API and the library and compares outputs.
- DHIS2 export (SCRUM-43): monthly aggregates per district in `dataValueSets` JSON, with a mapping file for data element and org unit identifiers (no invented identifiers), same suppression rule as open data.
- Open data (SCRUM-35): weekly counts per region (checks, findings per level, share of grey, unrecognised names), field dictionary, suppression of cells of 10 or fewer, test data labelled as such.
- Lighter voice model (SCRUM-42): distillation and quantisation scripts and an evaluation protocol; training needs a GPU and consented data.
- Audit (SCRUM-44): full-history secret and licence scan, report in `docs/audit/`.

## 12. Delivery process

For each ticket: implementer agent on its own branch with test-driven development, signed-off Conventional Commits with `Refs: SCRUM-<n>`; pull request with the template; a specification review and a code quality review by separate agents; fixes; merge with `tools/github/merge-pr.sh` once CI is green; Jira ticket moved to Done with the pull request link.

At the end of each phase, a live test round: several agents each play a real user (Bajenu Gox in accompanied mode using Wolof names, a literate woman alone on a 360 px phone, a midwife creating profiles at an antenatal visit, a pharmacy depot keeper at a sale, a safety auditor who tries to obtain a "safe" or green answer, an accessibility auditor, an airplane-mode tester) and drive the production build with Playwright. Findings are triaged, fixed in follow-up pull requests, and the round is repeated until no blocking finding remains. The Playwright MCP server is configured for the team in `.mcp.json`.

Testing on a phone in airplane mode needs HTTPS or localhost: `docs/testing.md` describes USB port forwarding (Chrome remote debugging, private) and a temporary HTTPS tunnel, then airplane mode after the first load.

## 13. Open questions for the clinical committee

Engineering defaults chosen as the conservative option, to be confirmed or changed by the pharmacist and the midwife of the committee. None of them is clinically validated. Questions 1 to 6 come from the safety review of SCRUM-45.

1. **Term look-ahead of 28 days** (`TERM_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS`, engine; 14 until the live test round 2, SCRUM-52). A rule that gives a finding is looked up with the upper end of the term moved on by 28 days, because a course of treatment goes on over the coming days and the next antenatal contact may be four weeks away. Is 28 days the right margin, and should it differ per rule (a single dose against a course of several days)? Consequences pinned by reference cases: misoprostol with a scar red from 8 weeks, NSAIDs before the next dose from 16 weeks, castor oil with a scar red from 26 weeks (committee question R-3 of `knowledge/README.md`).
2. **Findings reached only through the look-ahead.** Such a finding is held at before the next dose, never a danger (castor oil with a uterine scar at 26 to 29 weeks, misoprostol with a scar at 8 to 11 weeks), and its card says when the window is reached ("Elle atteindra 30 semaines dans 4 semaines au plus : ce produit demande alors plus de prudence."; "peut-etre ... dans les 4 semaines a venir" for an estimated term): is this hold and wording right?
3. **Estimated terms near term.** Should an estimated (month) term become outdated on its upper end, or should breastfeeding rules apply as possible instead of blocking (the interim default: possibly past term once the upper end is past 42+0 weeks, blocking only once the lower end is)? Is a month 9 answer (36 to 42 weeks) acceptable at all, or should weeks or the last period date be required near term (the app only suggests them for month 8 or 9)?
4. **How long "Je ne sais pas" holds.** How long does a "Je ne sais pas" answer (to "Enceinte ?" or to the term) stay valid before it is asked again? Interim default: possibly past term after 28 days (`UNKNOWN_HOLDS_DAYS`), blocking after 42 weeks.
5. **After the birth, without breastfeeding.** After "Oui, le bebe est ne" with "Allaite : Non", the app no longer covers her (it offers to delete the profile). Are postpartum women who do not breastfeed out of scope?
6. **Reminders and the look-ahead.** Is it right that the IPTp reminder is not shown at 10 to 11 weeks (reminders and notes do not use the look-ahead, so SP is never asked about before it can be given)?
7. **Unknown baby age read as the youngest band.** Every age-bounded breastfeeding rule applies when the baby's age is not known, when a pregnancy may be past term, and when the phone's clock is set back on a pregnancy profile.
8. **No "possibly past" stage for a last period date.** A last period date gives an exact term, so its lower and upper ends are the same day: it stays "ok" up to 42+0 weeks and blocks from 42+1, with no "A confirmer" stage. Weeks given today are a range of N+0 to N+6, so "42 weeks" given today is already "A confirmer" (its upper end is past 42+0) and blocks only once its lower end is past 42+0. This is consistent with the rule (possibly past term on the upper end, blocking on the lower end); is it the behaviour the committee wants?
