# Medical disclaimer

## Intended use

SIAPSO Mother-Child provides general health information, education and referral for pregnant and breastfeeding women in Senegal and the people who accompany them. It does not diagnose, prescribe or set a dose, and it does not replace a health professional.

The wording above is the shared source in [docs/intended-use.md](docs/intended-use.md). The README and the About screen of the app say the same.

## What the tool does

- It compares the medicine or remedy you name with a knowledge base of rules, each citing a published source.
- It shows how urgently the product should be shown to a health worker, and says who to show it to.
- When there is no reliable data, it shows a grey "unknown" state. Unknown never means safe.

## What the tool never does

- It never says that a product is safe, free of risk or verified.
- It never tells anyone to start, stop or change a treatment.
- It gives no dose and no alternative product.
- It never states that a plant treats a disease.
- It does not replace a consultation, an examination or the advice of a midwife, a nurse, a doctor or a pharmacist.

## In an emergency

Danger signs (for example heavy bleeding, convulsions, severe headache with blurred vision, high fever, reduced or absent baby movements) need a health worker now. Go to the nearest health post or health centre, or call the emergency number of your area. Do not wait for the app.

## Clinical validation status

The clinical content is not clinically validated today. A rule is marked validated only after a double review by a pharmacist and a midwife (see [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)). No rule has been through that review yet, and the knowledge pack declares `clinically-validated: false`. No partner, authority or health institution has endorsed this tool.

## Limits of the data

- The knowledge base is incomplete. A product that is not listed is not safe because it is not listed.
- Sources can be out of date. Each rule shows its source and the version of the knowledge base.
- Voice input can mishear a product name. The user always confirms the product, and the rules, not the speech model, decide the level.
- Strength and timing depend on the person. Only a health professional can judge them.
