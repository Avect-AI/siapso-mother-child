# Prescription reading evaluation

`pnpm eval:prescription` runs the reading of the app (`app/src/domain/prescription-reading.ts`: text lines in, products of the base out) on the text that OCR engines gave for 36 synthetic prescriptions, and on their exact text, and scores it against what each prescription says. It fails CI on a false product above the baseline, on a recall below the baseline, on a wrong strength or a wrong count per day offered on printed pages, on fewer products outside the pack kept as lines to add than the baseline, on any header line offered as typed or quoted, on a combination read in part without its warning, and on a header, stamp, place or date line that gives anything. Design and measures: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-05-prescription-scan-design.md` (SCRUM-57).

`pnpm eval:prescription:fresh` measures the reading on the fresh held-out set (`fresh/`, below), with no bar.

No image is read here and none is committed: only text. The prescriptions are synthetic (generated by `tools/prescription-eval/`, synthetic names, no real patient, no real prescription).

The OCR texts were made on 2026-11-05 by the spike of the spec (section 4), with Chromium 156, from the images of `node tools/prescription-eval/generate.mjs <out-dir> 36 <fonts-dir>`; the images were never committed. The source of each text is the `id` of its prescription and the `variant` of the image. The exact text (`text` source) is `truthText` of `synth.mjs`, computed at run time. The texts are as the engines gave them, but for one change: the long dashes Tesseract read in rules and strokes are written as en dashes (U+2013), since the repository holds no em dash; the reading treats both alike, and the figures are the same.

## What is measured

For each source, by set (`dev`, `heldout`, `fresh`), by image (`scan`, `photo`, `hard`, `dim`, `pdf-text`, `pdf-scan`) and by writing (`print`, `hand`, the handwriting-style proxy):

- **recall**: the products of the pack found, among those on the prescriptions; **precision**: the right products among those proposed. A product is right when its substances are those of a product of the prescription.
- **false products**: proposals whose substances are those of no product of the prescription, with those that are a part of a combination of the prescription ("part") and how many of them carry the warning that the line names more or that the product holds more ("warned"), and those read on the line of a product outside the pack ("out").
- **strengths**: the strength read on the line equals the one written, or differs ("wrong"); none read is neither (she is asked).
- **units read**: the units per day the posology reader gives for every product found; **offered**: what the list offers, for the products a daily-maximum rule watches only (`needsUnitsPerDay`), right or wrong. A posology is never used as a dose.
- **kept**: products outside the pack kept as lines to add as typed.
- **noise**: products read right that say their line names more (a warning she looks at for nothing).
- **header**: lines offered as typed, and lines quoted under a product, that hold a word of the header of the prescription (the patient's or the prescriber's name, a place, the practice), its date or an age. A name read from the paper must never reach an output.
- **must-warn**: what the reading made of each case: read whole, read in part with the warning, kept as typed, or failed (silent, a false product, lost, pre-tickable though unsure). **negative**: the lines that give nothing.
- The time of a reading per page (not a bar: machines differ).

## Bars

- No false product on printed pages, with or without the warning, for the engine the app ships and its paths (`paddleocr`, `pdf-text-layer`, `pdf-scanned-paddleocr`) and for the exact text: `maxFalsePrint` is 0 for them.
- On handwritten pages (the proxy), no more false products without the warning than the baseline (`maxSilentHand`, 0 for the engine the app ships): a combination read in part that says what else its line names is not counted there, since it is never pre-ticked and its line is offered as typed.
- No wrong strength and no wrong count per day offered on printed pages, for every source (`maxWrongStrengthPrint`, `maxWrongOffersPrint`: 0). A strength or a count the OCR may have garbled is not proposed: she is asked.
- No recall below the baseline, and no fewer products outside the pack kept as lines to add (`keptPrint`, `keptHand`), on printed and on handwritten pages, for every source.
- No header line offered as typed or quoted, on any page of any source (no baseline: always 0).
- Every must-warn case read whole or with its warning; every negative case gives nothing.
- The Tesseract sources may not do worse than their baseline: they are not shipped, they measure the reading on noisier text.

A baseline is changed only in a reviewed pull request that says why. When the reading improves, raise the bars to the new figures.

## How held out the sets are

The 36 prescriptions are split into `dev` (rx01 to rx24) and `heldout` (rx25 to rx36), but the held-out set was tuned on: rules of the reading were added after its failures (the joining of a name wrapped in a table cell came from what PaddleOCR made of it, spec section 4.5), unit tests use its text (the garbled sulfadoxine "ulfaderne" of rx33), and the rules added after the review of PR 70 were checked against its texts too. Its figures are therefore no held-out measure: like the development set, it shows what the rules were set to do. The baselines equal the figures measured today on both.

The set that was never tuned on is the fresh held-out set below; it is not independent of the rules either (see there).

## Fresh held-out set

Made on 2026-11-06, after the review of PR 70, by `tools/prescription-eval/fresh.mjs`, and frozen (`fresh/truth.json`, `fresh/pages.json`, written by `tools/prescription-eval/freeze-fresh.ts`) before the reading was run on it.

It was not tuned on: no rule was changed for what it showed. But it is not independent of the rules: it was designed after the review, by the same person who fixed the reading, around the cases the review raised (a pharmacy stamp, "Visa du pharmacien", a "City, le date" line, a postal address, a date in words, posologies after a dash and in brackets), with the same catalog of products and posologies as the 36 prescriptions. Its figures say that the rules hold on new layouts made with those cases in mind, not how the reading does on prescriptions nobody had in mind; real prescriptions, and an OCR measure, are the test of that.

What it holds:

- 12 prescriptions, fx01 to fx12: 8 printed, 4 in a handwriting-style font; 45 product lines, 28 of them in the pack and 17 outside it.
- New seeds (`424242 + 104729 i`, the development set uses `1000 + 7919 i`), the same catalog of products and posologies.
- New layouts: a regional hospital (bullet lines, the posology after a dash, "Thiès, le 06/11/2026", the patient with her age); a polyclinic letterhead ("1)" marks, the posology in brackets, a postal address, the date in words, "Madame" before the name); a table stamped by a pharmacy ("Traitement | Mode d'emploi", the pharmacy stamp, "Visa du pharmacien", the date line).
- New synthetic names (first and family names, prescribers, districts), some of which the reading does not list as names.
- Its exact text (`freshText`) is the whole sheet in reading order, header, signature and stamp included, unlike `truthText` of the 36 prescriptions.

Measured on 2026-11-06 at the end of the first round of the review of PR 70, then again after its second round (garbled brands, the header cut), with the same figures both times (`pnpm eval:prescription:fresh`); no rule was set on them:

## Known limits

- The combinations Tesseract read in part without a warning: "Tardyféron B9" read "B7" or "B?" (TARDYFERON alone: the line names no other substance), "Fer : qd rtgue" whose folic acid became unreadable, and SP whose sulfadoxine became "hdorine" or "ubfaderre". A line that names nothing more cannot say it. PaddleOCR read none of these in part.
- A strength the OCR misread into another plausible one ("flucinazole 100 mg" for 150 mg) is proposed: the strength is shown with the line it was read on, to compare with the box (spec, section 7.5).
- "Fe" for iron is not read as a substance name.
- A name read from the paper is recognised only as a header word, a title, a date, an age, or one of the common first and family names the reading lists: a numbered line holding only a name outside that list is still offered as typed.
- The set is synthetic, its photos simulated, and its handwriting only a font: real prescriptions will read worse (spec, section 4.8).
